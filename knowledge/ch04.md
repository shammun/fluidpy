## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
The chapter is one idea applied three times (mass, momentum, energy): **write the law for a material volume, let a
control volume coincide with it for an instant, use the Reynolds transport theorem to move it to any box, then Gauss +
localisation to get the law at a point.** A running ledger of equations vs unknowns (0 → 6 < 13 → 4 < 5 → 5 = 5 → 7 = 7)
threads the chapter.
1. **C01 Mass in a moving box (4.5).** d/dt∫_V ρ dV = 0 *(4.1)*; at the coincidence instant the ∂ρ/∂t integrals and the
   fluxes of V and V* are equal (4.4), so d/dt∫_{V*}ρ dV + ∫_{A*}ρ(u − b)·n dA = 0 *(4.5)* for any b (D01): b = u gives
   (4.1), b = 0 a fixed box. What crosses the wall is fluid *relative to the wall*.
2. **C02 Continuity (4.7)–(4.10).** Gauss turns the flux into ∫∇·(ρu) dV; "zero for every volume" + a continuous
   integrand ⇒ ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρu) = 0 *(4.7)* (D02, the localisation lemma with its small-ball proof). The product rule gives
   (1/ρ)Dρ/Dt + ∇·u = 0 *(4.8)*; incompressible means Dρ/Dt = 0 *(4.9)* ⇒ ∇·u = 0 *(4.10)* (D03) — weaker than
   ρ = const (a stratified shear flow is incompressible with ρ(z)). Gases behave incompressibly below M ≈ 0.3.
3. **C03 The 2-D stream function.** Steady continuity is solved identically by ρu = ∇χ × ∇ψ *(4.12)*; χ = −z gives
   ρu = ∂ψ/∂y, ρv = −∂ψ/∂x, ψ constant on streamlines and **flux between streamlines = ψ₂ − ψ₁** (D04), so crowded
   contours mean fast flow. Axisymmetric (Stokes) ψ: ρu_R = −(1/R)∂ψ/∂z, ρu_z = (1/R)∂ψ/∂R.
4. **C04 Forces from fluxes (4.17).** The coincidence trick on Newton's law *(4.13)* gives d/dt∫ρu dV + ∫ρu(u − b)·n dA
   = ∫ρg dV + ∫f dA *(4.17)* (D05). Wake drag F_D/l = ρ∫U(U∞ − U)dy (Ex. 4.1; mass leaks out of the sides), a bore
   moving at U → √(gh) (Ex. 4.3, ride with it and it is steady), a rocket (Ex. 4.4, accelerating CV), a jet on a plate, a
   sprinkler's torque (4.64)–(4.65). Drag is on the body; the fluid feels −F_D.
5. **C05 Bernoulli along a streamline (4.19).** A thin stream-tube element with first-order changes (and the easily
   forgotten side pressure force on the widening wall) gives ½U² + gz + p/ρ = const along one streamline for steady,
   inviscid, constant-density flow (D06) — pitot tubes, stagnation and dynamic pressure, Torricelli √(2gh).
6. **C06 Cauchy's equation (4.24).** Gauss on both surface integrals (the stress's **first** index sits on the normal,
   f_j = n_iτ_ij), localisation → flux form *(4.22)*, subtract u_j × continuity *(4.23)* → ρDu_j/Dt = ρg_j + ∂τ_ij/∂x_i
   *(4.24)* (D07). Newton for every continuum; 6 equations for 13 unknowns.
7. **C07 The Newtonian law (4.31).** A cube with τ₁₂ ≠ τ₂₁ would spin up as 6(τ₁₂ − τ₂₁)/(ρh²) → ∞, so τ is symmetric
   *(4.25)* (D08). τ = −pδ + σ *(4.27)*; σ depends only on S (a rigid rotation makes no stress); linear + isotropic
   K_ijmn = λδ_ijδ_mn + μδ_imδ_jn + γδ_inδ_jm *(4.29)* collapses 81 coefficients to two:
   τ_ij = −pδ_ij + 2μS_ij + λS_mmδ_ij *(4.31)* (D09 ★★★; "γ = μ" really names the only sum μ + γ that acts on a
   symmetric S). With μ_v = λ + ⅔μ: τ = −pδ + 2μ(S − ⅓S_mmδ) + μ_vS_mmδ *(4.37)*; Stokes' assumption is μ_v = 0 (D10);
   p − p̄ = μ_v∇·u *(4.34)*. For a parallel flow it is τ = μ du/dy *(1.3)*.
8. **C08 Navier–Stokes (4.39b).** (4.37) into (4.24) gives (4.38) (D11); constant μ, then ∇·u = 0 give
   ρDu/Dt = −∇p + ρg + μ∇²u *(4.39b)* (D12); the viscous force is also 2μ∂S_ij/∂x_i and −μ∇×ω *(4.40)* (D13) — solid
   body rotation has vorticity but no viscous force. μ = 0: Euler (4.41). Eight exact solutions check every term.
9. **C09 Rotating frames (4.45).** A turning basis has de′/dt = Ω × e′, so u = U + u′ + Ω × x′ *(4.42)* (D14);
   differentiating again produces **two** Ω × u′ (turning basis + d(Ω × x′)/dt) — the Coriolis factor 2 (D15 ★★★):
   acceleration terms +2Ω × u′ and +Ω × (Ω × x′) *(4.43)*; on the force side −2Ω × u′ (Coriolis) and −Ω × (Ω × x′) =
   Ω²R e_R (centrifugal) *(4.45)* (D16). A ball thrown from the pole deflects by Ωut² (D17); the centrifugal potential
   −½Ω²R² folds into effective gravity (D18). NH deflection to the right; flow round highs and lows; f = 2Ω sin φ named.
10. **C10 Energy and dissipation (4.57)–(4.58).** First law for a material volume → total energy (4.53) (D19, D20); the
    mechanical part is u · Cauchy *(4.56)* (D21); subtracting leaves De/Dt = −p Dv/Dt + (1/ρ)σ_ijS_ij − (1/ρ)∂q_i/∂x_i
    *(4.57)* (D22). The middle term is ε = 2ν(S − ⅓S_mmδ)² + (μ_v/ρ)S_mm² ≥ 0 *(4.58)* — a sum of squares (D23): a
    one-way pipe from kinetic energy to heat; entropy production k|∇T|²/(ρT²) + ε/T ≥ 0 *(4.63)*; 7 = 7.
11. **C11 The Bernoulli function (4.71).** The Lamb identity (u·∇)u = −u × ω + ∇(½u²) *(4.68)* and a barotropic pressure
    function turn Euler into ∂u/∂t + ∇B = u × ω *(4.69)* (D24); steady ⇒ B constant along streamlines **and** vortex
    lines *(4.71)*, everywhere if ω = 0 *(4.72)* (D25). The energy form h + ½|u|² + gz *(4.78)* is a different statement.
    A decision table sorts the four Bernoulli equations by their hypotheses.
12. **C12 Unsteady Bernoulli (4.75).** With u = ∇φ the Lamb term vanishes, the bracket depends on t only and is absorbed
    by φ_new = φ − ∫B dt′ (**the book prints +, which doubles B**) (D26): ∂φ/∂t + ½|∇φ|² + ∫dp/ρ + gz = const *(4.75)*
    — U-tube, accelerating sphere (added mass ½ρV), Ch. 7's free-surface condition.
13. **C13 Boussinesq (4.86), (4.89).** Subtract the hydrostatic base state; αδT ≪ 1 means density matters only next to g:
    Du/Dt = −∇p′/ρ₀ + (ρ′/ρ₀)g + ν∇²u *(4.86)* (D27); the pressure work p∇·u = ρR DT/Dt of a perfect gas turns C_v
    into C_p: DT/Dt = κ∇²T *(4.89)* (D28). Valid while αδT ≪ 1, L ≪ c²/g (11.8 km for air), low Ec and M.
14. **C14 Edges (4.91).** A surface η(x, t) = 0 made of fluid keeps η = 0 on each particle: Dη/Dt = 0 *(4.91)*, i.e. no
    relative normal velocity (D29); pillbox jumps (mass flux, traction, heat flux continuous), no-slip, no temperature
    jump; surface tension adds Δp = σ(1/R₁ + 1/R₂) *(1.5)*, now derived from a cap force balance; capillary length
    ℓ_c = √(σ/Δρg) ≈ 2.7 mm for water; meniscus h² = 2σ(1 − sin θ)/(ρg).
15. **C15 Similarity (4.101).** Scale by the problem's own sizes and divide by ρU²/l (D30):
    [Ωl/U]∂u*/∂t* + (u*·∇*)u* = −∇*p* + [gl/U²]g* + [μ/ρUl]∇*²u* *(4.101)*. Equal St, Fr, Re (and shape) ⇒ the same
    flow: sphere drag collapses onto one C_D(Re); a ship model cannot match Re and Fr at once (Ex. 4.8).

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only)
- **Test every scenario's full balance, not just its totals.** The oblique jet split 50/50 for every θ (should be
  Q(1 ± cos θ)/2 from along-plate momentum): mass and normal-force totals closed, so 147 tests passed; the derivation
  review caught it. Fix: each scenario reports `residual_momentum_along`; a test derives the split by hand.
- **Plane (2 × 2) tensors hide physics**: p̄ = −⅓τ_ii needs τ₃₃ = −p + (μ_v − ⅔μ)∇·u; −tr τ/2 gave p − p̄ = 0.833 where
  (4.34) requires μ_v∇·u = 0.5. `mean_pressure` now raises without `tau33`. Likewise the plane dissipation keeps the
  (3, 3) deviator −S_mm/3 (dropping it fails a discrimination test).
- **Series solutions**: never stop at the first zero coefficient (even Couette modes vanish by symmetry — stopped at
  n = 5, error 3e-3) and never project with a fixed-node quadrature (aliasing at n ≳ 100 gave 180 % error at t = 0):
  compute b_n in closed form and sum all modes (F1, loop 2; t = 0 error 2e-7 ΔT_max at 200 terms).
- **A scalar where a vector is expected must raise**, not become a z-component (`dU_dt`); 2-vector inputs are padded
  explicitly (review Should-fix 1).
- **Pin the index and sign conventions by discrimination**: Cauchy with the second index (1 test fails), Coriolis without
  the 2 (2), centrifugal with the centripetal sign (2), stream function sign (3), Lamb vector ω × u (1), outflux with u·n
  instead of (u − b)·n (6), Stokes assumption as λ = 0 (5), (4.29) third term duplicating μ (needed a new component
  check — contracting K with a symmetric S cannot see it).
- **Book typos are coded corrected and tested both ways**: (4.15)'s "= 0" (identity test), (4.51)'s dA (box Gauss with

## 5. Conventions used (see `knowledge/notation.md`)
- Dimensional SI; z up, g = −g e_z, Φ = gz (4.18). Non-dimensional work through `core.similarity.Scales`, which records
  its time scale (1/Ω as in (4.100), l/U as in (4.109)) and pressure scale (ρU², μU/l, ρgl).
- Outward n on every CV; flux with the **relative** velocity (u − b)·n; drag on the body positive downstream, on the
  fluid −F_D.
- τ = −pδ + σ, tensile positive; traction f_j = n_iτ_ij and Cauchy's divergence ∂τ_ij/∂x_i on the **first** index
  (`tensor_divergence(index=0)`; ch02's default is the second).
- Stokes assumption μ_v = 0 (not λ = 0); λ = μ_v − ⅔μ; σ "deviatoric" but traceless only if μ_v = 0 or ∇·u = 0.
- Rotating frame: primes = noninertial frame; **acceleration** +2Ω × u′, +Ω × (Ω × x′) (4.43) vs **force** −2Ω × u′,
  −Ω × (Ω × x′) = Ω²R e_R (4.45); NH Ω_z > 0, deflection to the right; `OMEGA_EARTH` = 7.292115e-5 rad/s (WGS-84).
- Boussinesq primes = perturbations from the hydrostatic base; buoyancy b = −gρ′/ρ₀ up-positive; lapse rates and N² in
  Kundu's Γ ≡ dT/dz with the meteorological form alongside (N114, N137).
- 2-D ψ: u = ∂ψ/∂y, v = −∂ψ/∂x (GFD texts often the opposite); axisymmetric ρu_R = −(1/R)∂ψ/∂z.
- Ro = U/(2Ωl) (forward pointer; Ch. 13 uses U/(fL)).
- Changes vs earlier chapters: primes now mean the rotating frame (§4.7) and perturbations (§4.9) — ch02 rotated axes,
  ch03 translating frame; σ = viscous stress (ch01 surface tension, ch03 core radius; §4.10 σ is surface tension again);
  ε = dissipation (ch02 alternating tensor, still used in (4.40)); γ = the third coefficient of (4.29) and Ex. 4.7's ζ/δ
  (ch01 ratio of specific heats, ch03 shear rate); Φ = force potential; B = Bernoulli function (ch03 line-vortex strength);
  C_p = pressure coefficient and specific heat; λ = second viscosity (ch02 eigenvalue) and model scale; M = mass, torque
  and Mach number; h = enthalpy, depth, heads, cube side; tensor divergence default index flipped for Cauchy.
