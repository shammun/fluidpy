## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
The chapter is one move used again and again: **pick a geometry whose symmetry kills the advective term, so the
Navier–Stokes equation (8.1) Du/Dt = −(1/ρ)∇p + ν∇²u becomes linear; integrate what is left with the wall conditions.**
When no symmetry is exact, **scale the equations until one balance dominates** (lubrication: pressure vs cross-gap
friction; creeping flow: pressure vs viscosity) — and when no length or time is imposed, **combine y and t into one
similarity variable**. Several profiles used as test fields since ch01–ch05 are finally derived here.
1. **C01 Laminar vs turbulent, ν as a diffusivity.** Reynolds's dye streak stays straight below Re = Ud/ν ≈ 2000–3000
   (diameter, mean speed); ν = μ/ρ [m²/s] is a momentum diffusivity with crossing time L²/ν (D01): air (ν ≈ 1.5e-5)
   spreads momentum 15.0× faster than water although water is ≈ 55× more viscous — the density ratio (≈ 830) wins.
2. **C02 Plane Couette–Poiseuille (8.5).** Fully developed ⇒ ∂u/∂x = 0 ⇒ v ≡ 0 (D02), so the momentum equations shrink
   to 0 = −(1/ρ)∂p/∂x + ν d²u/dy² (8.4a) and 0 = −(1/ρ)∂p/∂y (8.4b); "a function of x equals a function of y ⇒ both
   constant" makes dp/dx constant (D03); two integrations give u(y) = (U/h)y − (1/2μ)(dp/dx)y(h − y) (8.5) (D04) — a
   wall-driven line plus a pressure-driven parabola. **Backflow** at the fixed wall iff dp/dx > 2μU/h² (D05, ours), net
   flow Q = (Uh/2)[1 − (h²/6μU)dp/dx] reverses beyond 6μU/h²; the interior extremum sits at **y* = h/2 − μU/(h dp/dx)**
   (D05 steps 8–9; 7.00 mm for h = 1 cm, U = 1 cm/s, dp/dx = −0.5 Pa/m).
3. **C03 Pipe Poiseuille and Hagen–Poiseuille.** The same reduction in (R, φ, z) with (1/R)d/dR(R du/dR) and
   boundedness at the axis (drop A ln R) gives u_z = (R² − a²)(dp/dz)/(4μ) (8.6) (D06); τ = (R/2)dp/dz (8.7), wall stress
   τ₀ = (a/2)dp/dz (8.8) is a CV force balance πa²Δp = −2πaLτ₀ (valid for turbulent means too), Q = −πa⁴(dp/dz)/(8μ),
   u_max = 2V, Darcy f = 64/Re (D07, ours). Halving a loses 94 % of the flow. Re = 2000 at dp/dz = −1000 Pa/m ⇔ a = 2.00 mm.
4. **C04 Circular Couette (8.9)–(8.12).** The φ-equation is a pure viscous balance (no pressure, no advection):
   u_φ = AR + B/R (8.9), rigid rotation plus a free vortex, constants from the two walls (8.10) (D08, Euler–Cauchy ODE);
   limits R₂ → ∞ (the viscous but irrotational vortex Ω₁R₁²/R, (8.11), Γ = 2πΩ₁R₁²) and R₁ → 0 (rigid rotation Ω₂R,
   (8.12)) (D09); pressure from the centripetal balance dp/dR = ρu_φ²/R (ours); power in = −2πR₁σ_Rφu_φ = ∫ε dA
   (Exercise 8.12, V4); Rayleigh's criterion (Ch. 11) is a statement about A and B (stable iff Ω₂/Ω₁ > (R₁/R₂)²).
5. **C05 The lubrication approximation.** Two length scales x* = x/L, y* = y/εL, v* = v/εU (8.14) keep continuity
   coefficient-free (8.15); x-momentum carries inertia × **ε²Re_L** and ∂²/∂x² × ε², y-momentum only the pressure without a
   power of ε (8.16a,b) (D10 ★★★). For ε²Re_L ≪ 1: 0 ≅ −(1/ρ)∂p/∂x + ν∂²u/∂y² (8.17a, the page drops the ν), p independent
   of y (8.17b) (D11). Engine film ρ = 870, μ = 0.05, L = 5 cm, h = 50 µm, U = 5 m/s: Re_L = 4350 but ε²Re_L = 4.35e-3;
   with the book's p* = p/P_a the bearing number Λ = 49.35 — the natural pressure scale is μUL/h² = 5 MPa.
6. **C06 Lubrication profile, gap flux, Reynolds equation.** Integrating (8.17a) twice with x-dependent "constants"
   gives the local Couette + Poiseuille profile (8.19) (D12; coded with U₀(1 − y/h), the page adds U₀); integrating
   continuity across the gap with the Leibniz boundary term cancelling the kinematic wall condition gives
   ∂h/∂t + ∂q/∂x = 0 with q = −(h³/12μ)∂p/∂x + (U₀ + U_h)h/2 — the 1-D **Reynolds equation** the book leaves to exercises
   (D13 ★★★, ours). Constant gap in 2-D: Hele-Shaw ū = −(h²/12μ)∇p, ∇²p = 0 — viscous flow drawing ideal streamlines (N36).
7. **C07 The slider bearing (Example 8.1).** In the pad frame the flux is a constant C₁; integrating dp/dx = −12μC₁/h³ −
   6μU/h² with p = p_e at both ends gives the hump p − p_e = (6μLU/h₀²)·α(x/L)(1 − x/L)/[(2 + α)(1 + αx/L)²] and
   W = αμL²U/(2h₀²) (D14 ★★★, 16 steps; page slips: (1 − αx/L) integrands and a first-power denominator). Our exact load for
   any taper and optimum 1 + α = 2.18870 (San Andrés 2.1889, V5); the linear load is 15.6 % high at α = 0.1. W < 0 if αU < 0
   (suction). **Recirculation at the wide end iff the wide/narrow gap ratio exceeds 2 (α > 1 or α < −½).**
8. **C08 The thin-film equation (Example 8.3).** Hydrostatic p = ρg(h − y) (small slope), no slip below, no stress on
   top: a half-parabola with flux −(ρg/3μ)h³h_x; mass conservation gives h_t = (ρg/3μ)(h³h_x)_x (D15) — nonlinear diffusion
   that chokes itself as h → 0; volume is exact (2.2e-16 in the implicit solver).
9. **C09 Stokes' first problem.** No pressure gradient (still fluid far away), so u_t = νu_yy (8.20) (D16); five variables,
   two dimensions and linearity leave η = y/√(νt) (8.25) (D17); the chain rule (∂η/∂t = −η/2t) gives −(η/2)F′ = F″ (8.26)
   with F(0) = 1, F(∞) = 0 — three conditions collapse to two (D18); separation, the Gaussian integral and ξ = 2ζ give
   u/U = 1 − erf(y/2√(νt)) (8.30) (D19); δ₉₉ = 2 erfc⁻¹(0.01)√(νt) = 3.643√(νt) (8.31) (D20; the book's figures plot
   y/(2√(νt)), where the 1 % point is 1.82). ∫₀^∞ω dy = **+U** (page: −U).
10. **C10 The similarity ansatz (8.32).** γ = At^{−n}F(ξ/δ(t)): substitute, divide, demand every bracket scale with the same
    power of t, and let one conserved quantity fix the second exponent. Example 8.4: n = 0, δ = √(2C₁νt) (D21); Example 8.5:
    the velocity jump fixes n = ½, ω_z = −(U/√(πνt))e^{−y²/4νt}, u = U erf(y/2√(νt)), ±0.95U at η = ±2.772 (D22 ★★★), a
    temporally developing boundary layer with C_f = (2/√π)√(ν/U²t) ∝ Re_x^{−1/2} (1.1284); Example 8.6: the Lamb–Oseen vortex
    (stated, = ch03 Gaussian vortex with σ² = 4νt) and its spin-up twin; Example 8.7: n = m = 1/5 for the spreading bead from
    the conserved volume (D23; Huppert η_N = 1.411245, V5).
11. **C11 Stokes' second problem.** u = Re{e^{iωt}f(y)} (8.35) turns (8.20) into iωf = νf″ (8.36), k = ±(1 + i)√(ω/2ν),
    boundedness keeps the decaying root: u = Ue^{−y/δ_e}cos(ωt − y/δ_e), δ_e = √(2ν/ω) (8.38) (D24). Reading it (D25): 5.91 %
    left at the book's depth 4√(ν/ω) = 2√2 δ_e, crest speed √(2νω), wavelength 2πδ_e — **diffusion with a phase lag, not a
    wave** (no restoring force). Same (1 + i)/δ structure as the Ekman spiral.
12. **C12 Creeping flow.** The high-Re scaling (8.40) multiplied by Re loses the pressure (0 = μ∇²u, wrong); the dominant
    balance sets p − p∞ ~ μU/L, giving Re(u*·∇*u*) = −∇*p* + ∇*²u* (8.42) and, as Re → 0, the linear Stokes equations
    ∇p = μ∇²u (8.43) (D26). A 10 µm droplet: viscous pressure 0.022 Pa vs dynamic 1.7e-4 Pa.
13. **C13 Stokes' sphere solution.** The curl kills the pressure: ∇²ω = 0 (Cartesian components; spherical: −∇×∇×ω = 0)
    (D27); with ω_φ = −E²ψ/(r sin θ), (E²)²ψ = 0 (8.44) — the square of E², **not** ∇⁴ (D28 ★★★); f(r)sin²θ, the
    Euler–Cauchy roots {4, 2, 1, −1}, far field and no slip give ψ = Ur²sin²θ(½ − 3a/4r + a³/4r³) (8.48) (D29, 11 steps),
    velocities (8.49). θ from the **downstream** axis (rear stagnation θ = 0). Fluid-frame streamlines are fore–aft mirror
    images (reversibility); the disturbance decays only like a/r (side-line speed at 10a: 0.9248U vs ideal 1.0005U).
14. **C14 Stokes drag and settling.** Integrating ∇p = μ∇²u gives p − p∞ = −3μaU cos θ/(2r²) (8.50) (D30 ★★★; the rear
    minimum is **−**3μU/2a); the surface x-traction is the uniform 3μU/2a, collecting 2πμaU from pressure and 4πμaU from
    friction: D = 6πμaU (8.51) (D31 ★★★; a slip sphere would give 4πμaU). U_t = 2(ρ′ − ρ)ga²/(9μ); C_D = 24/Re (8.52),
    Re = 2aU/ν (diameter). 10 µm cloud droplet: U_t = 1.21 cm/s, Re = 0.016; 10 µm sand in water 0.36 mm/s; Millikan's
    balance recovers e (synthetic, 5.1e-4).
15. **C15 Where Stokes fails, and Oseen.** Far away inertia/viscous = **½ Re_a r/a** on the axis and at θ = π/2 (Re_a = ρUa/μ),
    so inertia catches up at r ≈ 2a/Re_a (D32; the book's "r/a ~ 1/Re" is right in order); Oseen keeps U∂u′/∂x: (8.53)
    reduces to (8.48) near the sphere (D33), meets no slip only to O(Re), has a downstream wake, and
    C_D = (24/Re)(1 + 3Re/16) (the literature's 3/8 on the radius). Stokes' paradox (cylinder) and Proudman–Pearson named.

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only)
- **A test that only covers the degenerate case hides a sign.** The Couette–Poiseuille extremum y* = h/2 − μU/(h dp/dx)
  was coded with +; the tests only used U = 0, where y* = h/2 either way (review M1). Fix: dense-grid test (20 001
  points, walls included) for U ≠ 0 and both signs of dp/dx, with an a-priori grid bound. Rule: **test every parameter
  that enters a formula at a non-zero value, with both signs.**
- **A regime flag checked at one location is a guess.** The slider "inlet backflow" flag tested only x = L; the true rule
  (pad-frame u/U = −s + 3(1 − h_m/h)s(1 − s), reversal where h > 1.5h_m, h_m = 2(1 + α)h₀/(2 + α)) is **wide/narrow gap
  ratio > 2**, i.e. α > 1 (at x = L) **or α < −½ (at x = 0)**. Found by an explainer builder whose independent JS
  `recirc()` scanned both ends; pinned by a brute-force 401 × 799 pad-frame sign scan. Rule: **derive a flag's criterion
  from the field, then brute-force it over the whole domain and the whole parameter range the UI allows.**
- **A missing direction test.** The Oseen ψ could have been mirrored θ ↔ π − θ (wake upstream) without any test failing.
  Now pinned by sympy (ψ solves E⁴ψ = (U/ν)∂ₓ(E²ψ) exactly; the opposite sign leaves ≥ 1e-3 of the term size) and by
  "fluid-frame |ψ| larger at θ < π/2". Rule: **every asymmetric solution gets a test that its asymmetry points the
  right way.**
- **A verifier's "correction" can itself be wrong — magnitudes of vectors, not one component.** Loop 1 compared the radial
  inertia with the radial viscous term and reported a far-field prefactor ≈ 1/8 (crossover 10–20 a/Re_a); the implementer
  showed |ν∇²u| = |∇p|/ρ is a vector too: the ratio of magnitudes → **½ Re_a r/a** on the axis and at θ = π/2 (crossover
  ≈ 2a/Re_a), checked by sympy and at r = 400a (0.4981/0.4991). Rule: **compare |a|/|b|, not a_r/b_r, and let the
  implementer challenge a verifier finding with an independent computation.**
- **Code the corrected form, keep the printed one as a named option that a test must fail**: `lubrication_velocity(form=
  "book")`, `slider_bearing(model="book")`, `lubrication_nondim_sympy(printed_8_13b=True)`, sympy keys for the (1 − αx/L)
  integrands, Oseen's +∂p/∂x_i and the biharmonic ∇⁴ψ. Nine printed slips became nine failing variants.
- **Keep the book's gradient sign and accept the earlier chapter's as a keyword**: `dpdx` (favourable < 0) with `G=` =
  −dp/dx for ch04 parity; a planted "G passed as dp/dx" flips the parabola and is caught.
- **Cancellation and limits**: exact slider load W(α) cancels for small α → series below 1e-3 (continuity 1e-8);
  erfc, never 1 − erf, at large η; Oseen's (1 − e^{−s})/s via −expm1 (exact at Re = 0); R₂ = ∞ and R₁ = 0 as exact
  branches (not 1e300); sphere fields NaN for r < a; t ≤ 0 guards (`stokes_first_vorticity(y, 0)` returned NaN).
- **Crank–Nicolson with a sudden wall jump oscillates** (pure CN stalls below order 1 in the pairwise study); two
  backward-Euler start-up steps restore order 1.99 — and a θ-scheme without its explicit half (θ = 1 by mistake) shows
  order 0.996, so the order test discriminates.
- **Degenerate nonlinear diffusion** (D ∝ h³ → 0 at the front): implicit backward Euler + Newton (same fixed point as
  Picard), a precursor film h_min, conservative faces; report the front by a threshold; compare with the similarity
  solution at t₀ + T when the run starts on it at t₀ (the clock starts at 0 — a verifier's false alarm).
- **Finite-difference tolerances from a written budget**: nested stencils carry ε·r/h_rel²; the ratio test's Richardson
  bound had to include the function's own round-off (5.18e-5 vs an a-priori 5e-5 that ignored it) — write the budget in
  the test, never loosen by feel.
- **Staircase boundaries make a Laplace solve first order** (Hele-Shaw disk, observed 1.11); state it rather than claim 2.
- **Validation labels**: "converged" only with an asserted order; Gauss–Legendre exact from n = 1 is analytic; a published
  formula reproduced is a form cross-check (analytic), not V5; a correlation bracket (Morrison between Stokes and Oseen
  for 0.1 ≤ Re ≤ 5) is benchmark evidence for the *bracket*, not for the drag law.
- **Re with the radius vs the diameter**: every drag function names its Re; Oseen 3/16 (diameter) = 3/8 (radius); a
  planted 3/8 on the diameter is caught.

## 5. Conventions used (see `knowledge/notation.md`)
- Dimensional SI. §8.2 channel: x along the plates, **y across, walls y = 0 (fixed) and y = h (moving at U)** (not ±h).
  Pipe and circular Couette: cylindrical (R, φ, z), **capital R** the cylindrical radius, radius a; inner R₁, Ω₁, outer R₂, Ω₂.
- **Pressure gradient sign**: functions take `dpdx` = dp/dx (favourable < 0 drives +x flow) with `G=` = −dp/dx (ch04's
  `plane_poiseuille`, `exact_solution`) as an alias.
- §8.3 lubrication: x along, y across the gap h(x, t), lower wall U₀(t), upper U_h(t); **Hele-Shaw switches to z across the
  gap** and φ becomes a velocity potential ū = ∇φ. Scaled pressure p* = p/P_a (absolute atmospheric) gives Λ = μUL/(P_a h²);
  the code also reports the viscous scale μUL/h² (the one that makes Λ = 1). Slider: gap h₀(1 + αx/L), pad frame vs ground
  frame (`frame=`); in `slider_gap_velocity` y = 0 is the moving pad side.
- §8.4: **η = y/√(νt)** (8.25); the figures' y/(2√(νt)) is `similarity_variable(half=True)`; δ₉₉ = 3.643√(νt) on η;
  Example 8.6 uses plane polar (r, θ) with θ the azimuth. Vortex sheet: jump 2U, u = ±U far away (ch05's γ = u_below −
  u_above = −2U).
- §8.5: **ω = oscillation angular frequency** (ch07 meaning), δ_e = √(2ν/ω) (e-folding) vs the book's 4√(ν/ω) = 2√2 δ_e.
- §8.6: spherical (r, θ, φ), **θ from the downstream +x axis** (rear stagnation θ = 0, front π — as ch06 (6.91), not ch03's
  θ from +z); `frame="body"` (sphere at rest in U e_x) vs `"fluid"` (stream subtracted, sphere moving to −x); **Re = 2aU/ν
  (diameter)** in (8.52)–(8.53); Re_a = ρUa/μ = Re/2 in the far-field ratio and in the literature's Oseen/Proudman–Pearson.
- Four Reynolds numbers in one chapter: pipe Ud/ν (diameter, mean speed), lubrication Re_L = ρUL/μ (passage length;
  the relevant group is ε²Re_L), generic ρUL/μ (8.40), sphere 2aU/ν; plus Re_x = Ux/ν in Example 8.5.
- g: `core.lubrication` (thin film) and `core.creeping` (settling) default to G0 = 9.80665; ch04/ch05/ch07 G_BOOK = 9.81
  (the chapter prints no g-dependent number).
- **Changes vs earlier chapters**: ω vorticity (§8.1, Ex. 8.5, §8.6 ω_φ) **and** frequency (§8.5) inside one chapter; η
  similarity variable (ch07 elevation, ch04 level set); ε fineness ratio h/L (ch07 ε² density ratio, ch04 dissipation);
  δ three meanings (diffusion thickness, time-dependent length scale δ(t), Stokes-layer depth); θ azimuth (Ex. 8.6) vs polar
  angle from the downstream axis (§8.6); φ azimuth vs Hele-Shaw potential; γ generic dependent variable of the ansatz
  (ch05 sheet strength, ch07 Stokes coefficient, ch01 c_p/c_v); C₁ flux constant (Ex. 8.1) vs proportionality constant
  (Ex. 8.4); D integration constant **and** drag; E² Stokes operator vs E electric field (Millikan) vs E constant (Ex. 8.7);
  n similarity exponent vs number of electrons; h gap vs film thickness; Λ bearing number (new); R capital cylindrical
  radius vs r spherical/plane-polar.
