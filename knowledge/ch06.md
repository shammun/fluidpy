## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
The chapter is one idea used ten ways: **if the flow is irrotational and incompressible, everything is a solution of
Laplace's equation, solutions add, and the body is whichever streamline you choose to call a wall.** Then: build bodies
from elements, get forces from the far field, carry flows through maps, solve on a grid, go to 3-D, let the body
accelerate.
1. **C01 The ideal-flow equations (6.1).** ∇·u = 0 and ρDu/Dt = −∇p (p measured from hydrostatic). With ω = 0 the
   viscous term μ∇²u = −μ∇×ω vanishes although μ ≠ 0 (D01), so only the no-through-flow condition can be kept (no-slip
   is dropped). Trust it outside thin attached boundary layers at large Re (Re = 6.7e5 → δ ≈ 1.2 mm); not in wakes;
   Kelvin (ch05 C03) keeps the outer flow irrotational. A Poiseuille control keeps μ∇²u = −G ≠ 0.
2. **C02 ψ, φ and point sources of Laplace.** ω_z = −∇²ψ (6.4) (D02), so irrotational flow is ∇²ψ = 0 (6.5) except at
   point vortices ∇²ψ = −Γδ(x − x′)δ(y − y′) (6.6); likewise ∇²φ = q (6.11). ln r is harmonic for r > 0 yet its flux
   through every circle is 2π (D03) — a vortex ψ = −(Γ/2π) ln r (6.8) and a source φ = (m/2π) ln r (6.15) are deltas.
   Equipotentials cross streamlines at right angles with |∇φ| = |∇ψ| (D04). Climate hook: ψ from ω is QG PV inversion.
3. **C03 Superposition; any streamline can be a wall.** Laplace is linear, so flows add; no through-flow
   ∂φ/∂n = 0 ⇔ ∂ψ/∂s = 0 (6.16) (D05); far field ∂ψ/∂y = U (6.17); pressure from Bernoulli everywhere
   p + ½ρ|∇φ|² = const (6.18). Far fields decay as R⁻² for closed bodies and R⁻¹ with net source or circulation.
4. **C04 The doublet.** A source–sink pair pushed together with 2mε = |d| fixed gives
   φ = −d·x/2πr² = (|d|/2π)cos θ/r (6.29) (D06; the ε² terms cancel, error order 2.01); **d points from sink to
   source**. Kit: corner flow ψ = 2Axy (6.24) and relatives, harmonic polynomials.
5. **C05 The half-body.** ψ = Ur sin θ + (m/2π)θ (6.31): stagnation at x = −m/2πU, dividing streamline ψ = m/2, width
   → m/U (h_max = m/2U) by the limit *and* by mass balance (D07). C_p = 1 − |u|²/U² (6.32) on the body is
   −(2k cos θ + k²), k = sin θ/(π − θ), zero where tan θ = −2(π − θ): 113.218° (D08, our addition).
6. **C06 The cylinder and d'Alembert.** Stream + doublet d = −2πUa² e_x: ψ = U(r − a²/r)sin θ (6.33), surface speed
   2U|sin θ|, C_p = 1 − 4 sin²θ (6.35) (D09); fore–aft symmetry ⇒ D = 0. Real flows separate (qualitative band only).
7. **C07 Circulation gives lift.** Add a **clockwise** vortex: ψ = U(r − a²/r)sin θ + (Γ/2π)ln(r/a) (6.36);
   stagnation at sin θ = −Γ/4πaU (6.38), merged at Γ = 4πaU, a free pair with r₊r₋ = a² beyond it (D10); the cross
   term 2UΓ sin θ/πa of (6.39) alone survives the pressure integral: L = ρUΓ (6.40), D = 0 (D11). Every Γ satisfies the
   same BCs — uniqueness needs the Kutta condition (Ch. 14).
8. **C08 Images and Example 6.1.** Vortex images flip sign, source images keep it (D12). A vortex beside a wall drifts at
   Γ/4πh and unsteady Bernoulli gives the wall pressure
   (p − p∞)/ρ = (Γ²/4π²)(η² − h²)/(η² + h²)², η = Γt/4πh: suction −ρΓ²/4π²h² when overhead, sign change at
   t = 4πh²/Γ, maximum at t = 4√3πh²/Γ (D13; our addition). Bottom-pressure recorders feel eddies this way.
9. **C09 The complex potential.** w = φ + iψ (6.42) analytic ⇔ Cauchy–Riemann (6.44) ⇔ both harmonic, and
   dw/dz = u − iv (6.45) (D14; the velocity is the *conjugate*). Corner w = Az^n (6.46): walls at θ = 0, π/n; speed
   ∝ r^{n−1} — infinite at a re-entrant corner (n < 1), a stagnation point for n > 1 (D15). Every element in one line
   (6.47)–(6.53).
10. **C10 Blasius and Kutta–Zhukhovsky.** Pressure integral → D = −∮p dy, L = ∮p dx (6.56) (D16); tangency
    (u + iv)dz* = (u − iv)dz on the body (the ± of parallel/antiparallel cancels) gives
    D − iL = (iρ/2)∮(dw/dz)²dz (6.60) (D17 ★★★), and the integrand is analytic, so the contour may be moved off the body.
    The far-field Laurent series leaves only the 1/z term: D = 0, L = ρUΓ (6.62) **for any body** (D18 ★★★).
11. **C11 Conformal maps.** δw = (dw/dz)δz (6.63): every small element is turned and stretched alike, angles kept except
    where dw/dz = 0 (D19). z = ζ + b²/ζ (6.65) sends |ζ| = b to a slit and |ζ| = a to an ellipse (6.67) with foci ±2b
    (D20); the flow (6.68) comes along if the inverse takes the **outside root**
    ζ = ½[z + √(z − 2b)√(z + 2b)] (D21 ★★★; numpy's principal √(z² − 4b²) returns the inside root for Re z < 0).
12. **C12 Laplace on a grid.** Half-point differences give ψ_{i,j} = ¼[ψ_{i−1,j} + ψ_{i+1,j} + ψ_{i,j−1} + ψ_{i,j+1}]
    (6.72), truncation (Δx²/12)ψ_xxxx (D22); the four-point system (6.73) as Aψ = b with ρ_J = ½, ρ_GS = ¼ (D23);
    sweeps to 1e-10: Jacobi 34, Gauss–Seidel 19, SOR 12 (ω_opt 1.0718). Example 6.2 converges at **order 4/3** because
    the 270° corner makes ψ ~ r^{2/3} (n = ⅔ in (6.46)).
13. **C13 Axisymmetric flow.** Stokes ψ: u_R = −(1/R)∂ψ/∂z, u_z = (1/R)∂ψ/∂R (6.75); the field equation (6.77) is
    **not** Laplace's (no complex variables) (D24); flux 2πdψ (6.78). The sphere: d = 2πa³U, surface speed (3/2)U sin θ,
    C_p = 1 − (9/4) sin²θ (6.91) (D25) — milder than the cylinder (2U, −3) because 3-D flow escapes sideways.
14. **C14 Bodies from axial singularities.** A uniform line sink plus a point source closes the airship (6.95) when
    Q = ak (D26); turned round, N unknown segment strengths with ψ = 0 at N body points (D27). Only the *moments*
    converge (strengths alternate in sign on a Rankine oval; cond grows to 10¹⁷); even N only for symmetric bodies.
    Source panels (our labelled extension) are exact on a circle, O(1/N²) on an ellipse.
15. **C15 The accelerating sphere.** d(t) = 2πa³u_s (6.97) (D28); unsteady Bernoulli with ∂/∂x_s = −∇ gives the
    surface pressure (6.105) (D29 ★★★): a fore–aft symmetric speed part (no drag) plus an acceleration part that
    integrates to F_s = −M du_s/dt, M = (2π/3)ρa³ = half the displaced mass (6.108) (D30), confirmed by kinetic energy
    ½ρ∫|∇φ|² dV = ½MU² (D31 ★★★). A released bubble starts at 2g; a 2-D cylinder carries its whole displaced mass.

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only)
- **Check an equation's normalisation before calling it a slip.** (6.82) was flagged as a book error by the analysis
  and tested against a hybrid form the book never prints; the review (M1) showed it is exactly r × the App. B
  divergence. Retracted everywhere; the test now asserts the printed form vanishes for (6.90) and equals r × App. B
  (sympy). Before "the book is wrong", compare the *whole* printed form with a reference form times every plausible
  factor (r, sin θ, ρ).
- **Real slips, taught corrected** (verified on rendered pages and by sympy): (6.61)'s 1/z² coefficient is
  −(Ud/π + Γ²/4π²), not (Ud/π − Γ²/4π²), plus a stray outer square (harmless: only 1/z survives); (6.104)'s middle
  bracket prints −u_s/a³ where +u_s/a³ is right (`printed_bracket=True` reproduces it, off by exactly u_s); (6.108)
  keeps a stray dφ; cross-reference slips in §6.3, §6.4, §6.5, §6.6; "first-order" central differences are second-order
  accurate; Example 6.2's loop index and Δψ unit.
- **A newton search needs seeds at the problem's own length scale.** F1: the half-body stagnation point was missed for
  m ≲ 3 m²/s because Newton on dw/dz = U + m/2πz converges only within |z + ℓ| < ℓ, ℓ = m/2πU, and the 9 × 9 start grid
  had spacing 0.75 m. Fix: rings of seeds at ℓ·(¼, ½, 1, 2, 4) round every singularity (ℓ = m/2πU, Γ/2πU, √(d/2πU)),
  deflation, +0 snap of on-axis roots; tested from m = 1e-4 to 200 m²/s. A double root (Γ = 4πaU) is located only to
  √(residual tolerance) — a property of the problem, not a loose tolerance.
- **Complex branches are physics.** Zhukhovsky's inverse must be ½[z + √(z − 2b)√(z + 2b)] (cut on the slit); numpy's
  principal √(z² − 4b²) returns the inside root at every left-half point off the slit (|ζ| = 0.37 instead of 2.70 at
  z = −3 ± 0.5i, b = 1). On the slit itself both roots have |ζ| = b (the D21 check excludes it: design G1). Half-body
  θ and vortex φ need rotatable cuts (`log_branch(cut=)`, `power_branch`) so the cut sits inside the body.
- **Singular points pollute convergence globally.** Example 6.2 converges at order 4/3 at *every* probe (1.36, 1.39,
  1.30), not "≈ 2 away from the corner": the 270° corner's ψ ~ r^{2/3} (n = ⅔ in (6.46)) spoils the whole grid. Second
  order is shown on a smooth problem (sin πx sinh πy: 1.995). Report the order with its reason; never loosen it.
- **Stopping rules on fine grids.** When ρ → 1 both the residual and the change per sweep are ≈ (1 − ρ) × the true
  error: on the ×4 contraction grid after 120 Gauss–Seidel sweeps the change is 1.2e-3 and the residual 6.2e-4 while the
  error is 5.6e-2. Use a tight tolerance (1e-10) on the residual, far below the accuracy wanted.
- **Symmetric collocation at odd N is singular.** For a fore–aft symmetric body the reflection gives A = −PAP; at odd N
  the symmetric strength subspace is one dimension larger than the antisymmetric ψ subspace, so A is exactly singular
  (body error 0.3–0.5 m before the fix). `axial_singularity_solve` warns and returns the minimum-norm least-squares
  (antisymmetric) k with `odd_symmetric=True`; even N unchanged; asymmetric targets (airship) solve normally.
- **Say what converges and what does not.** The axial method's segment strengths alternate in sign (±9.6 at N = 20,
  ±18.6 at N = 40 on a Rankine oval) while the dipole moment converges (1.4e-3 → 2.9e-6); cond₁ reaches 3.5e17 at
  N = 80 (cap N at 40). Constant-source panels are **exact on a circle at every N** (round-off), O(1/N²) on an ellipse
  (2.057), and first order off the body (0.93 → 1). The ellipsoid k_n converge at order 1.06.
- **One default ρ per medium.** `lift_per_span(U=10, Gamma_cw=2)` gave 20 N/m (ρ = 1.0) while `surface_pressure_force`
  gave 24 N/m (ρ = 1.2) (review S4). Now every 2-D force helper defaults to air 1.2 kg/m³ (pinned by one test across
  seven helpers); Example 6.1 and the §6.9 sphere family use water 1000; `flow_field_callables`/`ideal_flow_residuals`
  1000. Teaching code passes ρ explicitly.
- **A flux check that telescopes is not a conservation test** (S2): Σ Δψ across a section equals the BC difference by
  construction (V1 identity). The real V4 invariant is the discrete circulation round every control cell
  (−(ψ_E + ψ_W + ψ_N + ψ_S − 4ψ_P) → 3.3e-10 ≤ 4 tol when converged). Public tests must pin a book example's geometry and
  BCs (S3: a planted non-uniform outlet passed until a geometry test was added).
- **Components vs magnitude in a rotated problem** (S1): for a stream at angle α, D − iL = −iρUΓe^{−iα} — the x/y parts
  (−6.21, 23.18 N/m at 15°) are not "drag and lift"; return F_perp, F_par and the stream angle, and make `alpha=None`
  explicit instead of silently becoming 15°.
- **Deprecate, don't silently reinterpret** (S6): `sphere_potential_vector` read a scalar `d_vec` as the radius; now

## 5. Conventions used (see `knowledge/notation.md`)
- Dimensional SI; 2-D flows in the (x, y) plane with θ from +x (downstream); p measured from hydrostatic; D, L = forces
  **on the body** per unit depth, F in (6.54) on the fluid.
- **Γ: counterclockwise positive in all code** (`Vortex(Gamma)`, ψ = −(Γ/2π) ln r (6.8)); the book's (6.36)–(6.40),
  (6.52), (6.61)–(6.62), (6.68) and Example 6.1 use a **clockwise** Γ (the flow's circulation is −Γ, so L = +ρUΓ).
  Functions that follow the book take `Gamma_cw=` or `Gamma_ccw=` keywords (never a bare `Gamma`); `gamma_ccw_from`
  converts. Explainers show both ("Γ_cw 2 ⇔ Γ_ccw −2 m²/s").
- **Doublet**: the 2-D vector d = Σx_i m_i points from the sink to the source, φ = −d·x/2πr² (6.29); the cylinder needs
  d = −2πUa² e_x (upstream); (6.49)'s scalar d is a dipole −d e_x (`Doublet.from_book_scalar`). 3-D: (6.88) dipole
  −d e_z, sphere d = 2πa³U; moving sphere d(t) = +2πa³u_s.
- **Three angle origins**: θ from +x (downstream) in the polar formulas (upstream stagnation at θ = π); Fig. 6.10-style
  comparisons measure from the upstream stagnation point (π − θ); (6.106)'s θ_s from the sphere's velocity (θ_s = π − θ
  of (6.91)).
- **Complex potential** w = φ + iψ, z = x + iy = re^{iθ}, dw/dz = u − iv; the velocity vector is conj(dw/dz). ζ = the
  Zhukhovsky (circle) plane, z = ζ + b²/ζ; the outside root is the physical one.
- **§6.8 axes**: z is the **horizontal** symmetry axis along the stream (not up); R the distance from it; Stokes ψ in
  **m³/s** (plane ψ is m²/s), u_R = −(1/R)∂ψ/∂z, flux between stream surfaces 2πdψ. §6.9: ξ = x − x_s (in §6.8 ξ is the
  axial coordinate along the line sink), **w = the z-velocity** (not the complex potential).
- Laplace grids `mask[j, i]` (True = unknown), x along the last axis (project layout), Δx = Δy for the average rule.
- ρ defaults: 1.2 kg/m³ for 2-D force helpers, 1000 kg/m³ for Example 6.1, §6.9 and C01 residual tools.
- **Changes vs earlier chapters**: z is horizontal in §6.8 (up everywhere before); w is the complex potential in §6.4–6.6
  but a velocity component in §6.9; Γ is clockwise in half the chapter's equations; ζ is the Zhukhovsky variable (ch05
  relative vorticity, ch01 displacement); a is cylinder/sphere radius, half-body stagnation distance and airship
  line-sink length; n is the corner exponent and the unit normal; k is the line-sink strength and the half-body ratio
  sin θ/(π − θ); d is the doublet strength (ch05 segment distance); M is added mass (ch05 segment count); m is source
  strength (ch05 elliptic parameter).
