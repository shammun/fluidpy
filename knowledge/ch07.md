## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
The chapter is one machine used eight times: **write the fields as a plane wave e^{i(kx − ωt)}, let the boundary (or
interior) conditions turn the PDEs into algebra, and read off ω(k) — the dispersion relation. Everything else (speeds,
orbits, energy, groups, rays) is read from ω(k).** Then linearity breaks (jumps, Stokes drift, solitons) and the
restoring force changes (surface tension, a density step, continuous stratification).
1. **C01 Vocabulary (7.1)–(7.9).** η = a cos(kx − ωt) (7.2); a crest is "phase = 2nπ", so it moves at c = ω/k = λν
   (7.4) (D01); the plane wave a cos(K·x − ωt) (7.5) has crests 2π/K apart along e_K and c = (ω/K)e_K (7.8); trace
   speeds ω/k, ω/l, ω/m are each ≥ c and are **not** components of c (1/c² = Σ1/c_i²); a fixed probe in a current sees
   ω₀ = ω + U·K (7.9). Linear waves superpose (Fourier, `linear_evolve`).
2. **C02 The linear free-surface problem.** ∇²φ = 0 in −H < z < η, w = 0 on z = −H, the kinematic condition
   (∂φ/∂z)_{z=η} = ∂η/∂t + η_x φ_x (7.16) (D02, with f = z − η), and linear Bernoulli with p = 0 (7.20). Taylor-moving
   both conditions from z = η to z = 0 drops terms of **relative** size ka (D03, D04): (∂φ/∂z)₀ ≅ ∂η/∂t (7.18),
   (∂φ/∂t)₀ ≅ −gη (7.21). Measured: exact-condition residuals of the linear solution scale with relative slope 1.01
   (absolute slope 2.01 at fixed k) — the analysis' "(ka)² × aω" was wrong.
3. **C03 The dispersion relation (7.28).** The trial φ = f(z) sin(kx − ωt) (7.22) turns Laplace into f″ − k²f = 0,
   the bottom fixes B = Ae^{−2kH}, the kinematic condition the size, giving φ = (aω/k) cosh k(z + H)/sinh kH ·
   sin(kx − ωt) (7.26) (D05); the dynamic condition leaves ω² = gk tanh kH (7.28) (D06). ch04 had used this field as a
   given test case — now it is derived (parity 1e-12).
4. **C04 Phase speed and regimes.** c = √((g/k) tanh kH) (7.29) grows with λ: dispersive (D07). Deep (kH > 2 ⇔ H >
   0.318λ, "deeper than about a third of a wavelength"): c = √(g/k) (7.45), error 1.815 % at kH = 2 (D08); shallow (H <
   0.07λ): c = √(gH) (7.49), error 3.039 %, c/√(gH) ≈ 1 − (kH)²/6 (D09). The same cosh ratio gives the pressure
   p′ = ρga cosh k(z + H)/cosh kH · cos(kx − ωt) (7.31): e^{kz} decay in deep water (4.32 % at z = −λ/2), hydrostatic
   p′ = ρgη (7.52) in shallow water. Worked: T = 10 s deep → λ = 156.1 m; a 4 km ocean → √(gH) = 198 m/s.
5. **C05 Orbits.** Linearising the path lines about the mean position (D10) gives closed ellipses (7.36) with semi-axes
   a cosh k(z₀ + H)/sinh kH and a sinh k(z₀ + H)/sinh kH, a focal distance a/sinh kH independent of depth, all
   particles of a column in phase, **clockwise** for a right-going wave (D11); circles ae^{kz₀} deep (7.46), flat
   ellipses a/kH × a(1 + z/H) shallow (7.50); streamlines ψ (7.37) (u = ∂ψ/∂z).
6. **C06 Energy.** E_k = ½ρg⟨η²⟩ (7.39) (D12; ±H/2 terms cancel between cos² and sin² weights), E_p equal (7.41)
   (D13), E = ½ρga² (7.42) per unit horizontal area; the flux (7.43) → (7.44) is E times (c/2)(1 + 2kH/sinh 2kH),
   **not** c (D14) — the cliff-hanger for C09. Worked: T = 10 s, a = 1 m deep swell → 38.3 kW per metre of crest.
7. **C07 Capillary–gravity waves.** The Laplace jump on a curved surface, p = −ση_xx (7.54), adds (σ/ρ)η_xx to the
   dynamic condition (7.55); η_xx = −k²η turns every g into g + σk²/ρ (7.56)–(7.57) (D15). c(λ) has a minimum
   c_min = (4gσ/ρ)^{1/4} at λ_m = 2π√(σ/ρg) (7.58), where c_g = c (D16); clean water (σ = 0.07274 N/m, ρ = 998.2):
   **23.12 cm/s at 1.712 cm**; minimum group speed **17.76 cm/s at 4.354 cm** (computed, 4 s.f.).
8. **C08 Standing waves and seiches.** Two opposite waves give 2a cos kx cos ωt with ψ (7.62), u (7.63) (D17; the
   left-going ψ carries a minus); walls at x = 0, L allow λ = 2L/(n + 1) (7.64) and ω from (7.65) (D18). Our 2nd-order
   finite-difference sloshing eigenproblem converges to (7.65) at order 1.98 (independent of (7.28)).
9. **C09 Group velocity.** Beats give an envelope moving at Δω/Δk → dω/dk (7.66)–(7.67) (D19; the page prints ½Δω x —
   read ½Δω t); a narrow Fourier packet's envelope moves at c_g, the dropped ½ω″(k − k₀)² term spreads it (D20 ★★★);
   c_g = (c/2)[1 + 2kH/sinh 2kH] (7.69): c/2 deep, c shallow (7.70), and F = E c_g (7.71) (D21). Capillary waves have
   c_g = 3c/2; the stone in a pond leaves a calm disc of radius c_g,min t.
10. **C10 Kinematic waves and rays.** With local k = ∂θ/∂x, ω = −∂θ/∂t, crest conservation ∂k/∂t + ∂ω/∂x = 0 (7.74)
    and k is carried at c_g (7.75) (D22); in a steady, slowly varying medium ω is constant along rays (7.79) (D23); our
    Hamiltonian companion dk/dt = −∂ω/∂x bends the rays, and on a plane beach |k| sin α = const (Snell, D24, ours):
    T = 8 s, 30° at 20 m → 11.27° at 2 m; rays wrap round an island onto its lee.
11. **C11 The hydraulic jump (and the soliton).** Finite amplitude makes crests outrun troughs (our Riemann simple
    wave, labelled); the steepened front becomes a jump whose CV momentum balance gives H₂/H₁ = ½(−1 + √(1 + 8Fr₁²))
    (7.81) (D25) and whose energy change E₂ − E₁ = −g(H₂ − H₁)³/(4H₁H₂) forbids Fr₁ < 1 (D26). If dispersion balances
    steepening instead, KdV (7.87) has the solitary wave (7.88) with c = c₀(1 + a/2H) (sympy residual 0); the Ursell
    ratio aλ²/H³ decides (crossover computed from the two KdV terms, 4π²/9).
12. **C12 Stokes waves and drift.** Second harmonic ½ka² sharpens crests (7.82); c² = (g/k)(1 + k²a²) (7.83) needs the
    **consistent** third-order expansion (γ = 1; the tempting truncated set-up gives γ = 3/8). Orbits fail to close:
    the Taylor term ξu_x + ζu_z averages to ū_L = a²ωke^{2kz₀} (7.85) (D27; no ½, decay rate 2k), (7.86) at any depth;
    the Eulerian mean stays zero. Numeric drift from exact path lines → (7.86) at order 0.976 in ka.
13. **C13 Interfacial waves.** Complex amplitudes turn two deep fluids into algebra (D28): ω = ε√(gk) (7.95),
    ε² = (ρ₂ − ρ₁)/(ρ₂ + ρ₁); the fluids slide past each other as a vortex sheet of strength 2ωa cos θ (ch05 D23);
    E = ½(ρ₂ − ρ₁)ga² (7.96); ρ₁ > ρ₂ ⇒ imaginary ω (Rayleigh–Taylor, NaN + warning; Ch. 11).
14. **C14 Barotropic and baroclinic modes.** A layer of depth H over a deep fluid: the constants (7.104)–(7.109) (D29
    ★★★; the page's (7.105) prints kz for kx), the dispersion relation factors as (ω²/gk − 1){…} = 0 (7.110) (D30 ★★★),
    so ω² = gk (barotropic, b = ae^{−kH}) or (7.113) (baroclinic, η/ζ < 0 and tiny) (D31); long waves travel at
    √(g′H) with **g′ = g(ρ₂ − ρ₁)/ρ₂** (7.116)–(7.117): 0.99 m/s for H = 50 m, Δρ = 2 kg/m³ against ≈ 22 m/s for the
    surface mode. √(g′H)/f is Ch. 13's baroclinic Rossby radius.
15. **C15 Internal waves: ω = N cos θ.** Linearising Boussinesq about ρ̄(z) (D32) and eliminating to one equation
    ∂²/∂t² ∇²w + N²∇_H²w = 0 (7.134) (D33 ★★★, valid for N(z) too) gives ω² = N²(k² + l²)/K² (7.137), i.e.
    ω = N cos θ (7.139) (D34): frequency set by direction only, 0 < ω < N (ω = N is ch01's parcel oscillation).
16. **C16 Transverse waves, c ⟂ c_g, beams, F = c_g E.** K·u = 0 (7.141) (D35); c_g = ∇_K ω is perpendicular to c
    (7.146), horizontal parts share a sign, vertical parts are opposite — phase up, energy down (D36, sign-safe for
    k < 0); an oscillating source radiates four beams at arccos(ω/N) from the vertical (ω/N = 0.71 → 44.77°); polarization
    (7.153) and ½Re(AB*) give E_k = E_p and F = c_g E (7.159) (D37).

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only)
- **Complex-step derivatives need analytic continuations of every non-analytic piece.** `group_velocity_numeric` fed a
  complex k through `_abs_k`, which returned complex k unchanged; for k < 0 the step evaluated √(gk) on the wrong branch
  (c_g = 3.1e20 m/s, `gaussian_packet(k0=−0.1)` 9.9e19, no warning). Fix: |k| → −k where Re k < 0; `group_velocity`
  returns the **signed** dω/dk = sgn(k)(c/2)[1 + 2|k|H/sinh 2|k|H] while `phase_speed` stays |c| (review M1, two
  discriminating tests, 6/6 variants caught). `np.abs` in the continuation is not a fix (non-analytic → central
  fallback, loses ω″ precision).
- **Printed formulas that silently assume a sign.** (7.138) ω = kN/K and (7.145) c_g = (Nm/K³)(m e_x − k e_z) assume
  k > 0; the book's own Fig. 7.29 draws k < 0, where the printed c_g points the wrong way. Code ω = N|k|/K and
  c_g = ∇_K ω; test all four quadrants; θ from cos θ = |k|/K, never arctan(m/k).
- **A literal exercise set-up can give a different coefficient than the published result** (review M2): Stokes'
  c² = (g/k)(1 + γk²a²) has γ = 1 only from the consistent third-order expansion (the potential's first harmonic is
  corrected by −(ka)²/8 relative to a√(g/k)); the truncated ansatz gives γ = 3/8. Teach the consistent expansion, name
  the trap, never attribute γ = 1 to the truncated set-up.
- **Asymptotic orders are stated relative or absolute, not mixed.** Linear-solution residuals of the exact free-surface
  conditions: relative slope 1 (O(ka)), absolute slope 2 at fixed k (analysis V25 had "(ka)² × aω").
- **Overflow-safe hyperbolic ratios**: cosh k(z + H)/sinh kH = e^{kz}(1 + e^{−2k(z+H)})/(1 − e^{−2kH}) (exact, finite
  at kH = 500, e^{kz} automatically for H = ∞); x/sinh x via 2xe^{−x}/(−expm1(−2x)); numpy's complex tanh raises a
  spurious overflow flag for kH ≳ 355 (`np.errstate(over="ignore")`, asserted under `simplefilter("error")`).
- **Inverse dispersion by a physics bracket**: the deep root ω²/g and the shallow root ω/√(gH) bound k; brentq with the
  residual asserted inside the function; round trip 1e-12 over ω ∈ [1e-4, 1e3] rad/s × H ∈ [1e-3, 1e4] m and ∞.
- **Benchmarks: quote the primary source's number at its printed precision.** Fenton's 2006 note says 1.5 % / 0.7 %
  for Fenton & McKee / Guo; the primary papers say 1.7 % (ours 1.658 %) and 0.75 % for Guo's own β = 2.4908 (ours 0.753 %);
  the note's 1.5 % matches the *period* error, and 0.79 % belongs to the 5/2 rounding.
- **Sibling argument orders differ** (kept for API stability; documented, call by keyword): `omega_capillary_gravity(k,
  H, sigma, rho, g)` with σ = 0.0727 default vs `phase_speed/group_velocity/…(k, H, g, sigma, rho)` with σ = 0 default.
- **FFT evolution**: periodic domain long enough that the fastest component (c_g,max t) does not wrap; handle ω(0);
  packet centroid → c_g with error O(δk²) (order 2.0 measured).
- **KdV**: stiff third derivative → integrating factor; dealias 2/3; the soliton's tail must not sit on the periodic
  seam; invariants (mass 2e-16, momentum 7e-10, Hamiltonian 9e-10) are checked every run; the cnoidal *mean* datum
  converges only like 1/K(m) (use the trough datum for the m → 1 limit).
- **Stokes-drift numerics**: drift is O((ka)²) of the orbit — DOP853, rtol 1e-10, ≥ 10 periods; release particles at
  their **mean** depth (`particle_path(start="mean")`), not on the line (their mean sits lower by aS).
- **Two different reduced gravities are both "g′"**: (7.117) divides by ρ₂ (the lower fluid), ch04's `reduced_gravity`
  by ρ₁; they differ by ρ₂/ρ₁ (0.2 % in the ocean). `reduced_gravity_book(ref=)` names the choice; a test pins the ratio.
- **Rule 9 near-miss (public repo)**: a value we compute can round to an exercise's printed answer (c_g,min 17.76 cm/s
  printed to 3 s.f. is the exercise answer). Print 4 significant figures of computed values that coincide with exercise
  answers, build them from the function (never typed), and keep the book's value only in the private JSON.

## 5. Conventions used (see `knowledge/notation.md`)
- Dimensional SI; x along propagation, **z up**, still surface z = 0, bottom z = −H; `H = np.inf` = deep water. §7.7
  two-layer problem: origin at the mean free surface, interface at z = −H (H = upper-layer thickness).
- **η is the surface elevation** η(x, t); the level-set function is f = z − η (7.14). In ch04 (4.90) η *was* the
  level-set function — call `core.interfaces.kinematic_bc_residual` with z − η, never with the elevation.
- **ω is an angular frequency** [rad/s] (vorticity in ch02–ch06), ω ≥ 0; the sign of k gives the direction;
  `phase_speed` returns |c|, `group_velocity` the signed dω/dk. ω is intrinsic; the probe frequency is ω₀ = ω + U·K.
- p is **gauge**; p′ ≡ p + ρgz in §7.2 (7.30) but p′ = p − p̄(z) in §7.8 (7.124). ψ: u = ∂ψ/∂z, w = −∂ψ/∂x
  (ch04's planar convention with y → z; GFD's opposite sign is the Ch. 13 trap; a planted GFD sign fails a test).
- ζ is the particle's vertical excursion (§7.2, §7.6, §7.8) **and** the interface displacement (§7.7); θ is the local
  phase θ(x, t) (7.72) **and** the angle of K above the horizontal (7.139) (`theta_phase`, `theta_K`); the beam (c_g,
  particle motion) makes θ with the **vertical**. ε² = (ρ₂ − ρ₁)/(ρ₂ + ρ₁) (`eps2_density`), not a dissipation.
- ρ₁ = upper (lighter), ρ₂ = lower (heavier); **g′ = g(ρ₂ − ρ₁)/ρ₂** in (7.117) vs ch04's ρ₁.
- **Complex notation from §7.7**: fields are Re{A e^{i(kx − ωt)}} with Re dropped during linear algebra (∂/∂x → ik,
  ∂/∂t → −iω); take real parts before multiplying: ⟨Re(Ae^{iθ})Re(Be^{iθ})⟩ = ½Re(AB*). ρ′ lags w by 90° (factor i).
- Energy normalisations switch: surface and interfacial E, E_k, E_p per unit **horizontal area**, F per unit crest
  **length**; internal-wave E per unit **volume**, F per unit **area**; the jump energy E = u²/2 + gH is per unit
  **mass** (head loss ΔE/g).
- g = 9.81 (`G_BOOK`) in every ch07 and `core.waves` default (core elsewhere uses G0 = 9.80665); ρ = 1000 unless stated;
  explainers and the notebook's clean-water numbers use σ = 0.07274 N/m (IAPWS 20 °C) and ρ = 998.2 kg/m³.
- Vortex-sheet strength γ = u_below − u_above (ch05 convention); rays: α measured from the **shore normal**.
- **Changes vs earlier chapters**: ω frequency (was vorticity), η elevation (was level-set function), ζ particle or
  interface (was parcel displacement ch01, relative vorticity ch05, Zhukhovsky plane ch06), θ phase/K-angle (three angle
  origins in ch06), g′ with ρ₂ (ch04 ρ₁), m vertical wavenumber (mass, source strength, elliptic parameter before), ν
  cyclic frequency in `wave_parameters` (kinematic viscosity elsewhere, also in `viscous_decay`), T period (temperature),
  σ surface tension but `sigma_x` packet width, γ Stokes coefficient (sheet strength ch05, gas γ ch01), b interface
  amplitude (Zhukhovsky constant ch06, CV velocity ch04), E per area/per volume/per mass, Q discharge per width [m²/s]
  (ch06 3-D source [m³/s]), f the level set z − η (Coriolis parameter elsewhere), H depth or upper-layer thickness
  (scale height ch01, channel width ch05).
