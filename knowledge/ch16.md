## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
**Blood is a slightly odd fluid pushed in pulses through elastic, branching, curved and sometimes collapsing tubes — and
almost every answer is Poiseuille's resistance or a wave whose speed the wall sets, modified by one number α that compares
the vessel with its Stokes layer.** Four arcs inside §16.3: steady (C04, C05), storage and waves (C06–C08), oscillating flow
(C09–C11), geometry and odd materials (C12–C16); §16.4 re-uses Poiseuille and hydrostatics for plants (C17).
1. **C01 The heart as a pump.** $SV=EDV-ESV$ (16.1), $CO=SV\times HR$ (16.3); the ventricle traces a counter-clockwise loop
   in (V, p); the work per beat is $W=-\oint p\,dV$ (ours; D01, shoelace sum, trap T11) and $R=\Delta\bar p/Q$ (16.9) closes
   the circuit; the ⅓ rule $\bar p_A=\tfrac13p_S+\tfrac23p_D$ (16.11) is an estimate (−5.25 mmHg against the true mean of
   our Windkessel beat, T15).
2. **C02 Blood thins when sheared.** $\sqrt\tau=\sqrt{\tau_y}+K_c\sqrt{\dot\gamma}$ (16.6)/(16.207) is a straight line in √γ̇;
   μ_app = τ/γ̇ = (√(τ_y/γ̇) + K_c)² (D02) falls to K_c² and blows up at rest; secant ≠ tangent (T27); k_c is dimensionless,
   K_c = k_c√μ_p (T14).
3. **C03 One number for every vessel.** $\alpha=a\sqrt{\omega/\nu}$ (16.12) = √2 a/δ_e = ½√(Re·St) on the diameter forms (D03,
   T6): our aorta 15.9, artery 5.1, capillaries ≪ 1.
4. **C04 Resistance as a⁻⁴.** $R=\dfrac{\Delta\bar p}{Q}=\dfrac{8\mu L}{\pi a^4}$ (16.20) from (16.17) (D04): halving the radius ×16
   — why arterioles set blood pressure.
5. **C05 A plasma layer lubricates narrow tubes.** Two Newtonian layers matched at r = a − δ (D05–D07) →
   $\mu_{app}=\mu_p\left[1-\left(1-\dfrac\delta a\right)^4\left(1-\dfrac{\mu_p}{\mu_c}\right)\right]^{-1}$ (16.33); (16.30) printed wrong (S3).
6. **C06 An artery is a spring.** Hoop stress $\sigma_{\theta\theta}=(p-p_e)a/h$ (16.39) + Hooke → the tube law
   $a=a_0\left[1-\dfrac{a_0}{Eh}(p-p_e)\right]^{-1}$ (16.41) (D08), singular at a₀(p − p_e)/(Eh) = 1 (T16); the distensible
   tube's flux (16.46) with **a₀⁴** (S4).
7. **C07 Store and drain.** $Q(t)=\mathrm K\dfrac{dp}{dt}+\dfrac{p}{R}$ (16.49) (D09) and its integrating-factor solution (16.52)
   (D10) with the periodic state p(T) = p₀ (ours): RK is the diastolic time constant; a stiffer aorta raises the pulse
   pressure at the same mean.
8. **C08 The pulse is a wave of wall stretch.** Linearised (16.56)–(16.58) (D11; (16.54) corrected, S5) → $\dfrac{\partial^2p}{\partial t^2}=c^2\dfrac{\partial^2p}{\partial x^2}$
   (16.61), $c=\sqrt{\dfrac{Eh}{2\rho a_0}}$ (16.62) (D12); d'Alembert and p̂ = ρc û recapped from ch15; our aorta 5.6 m/s, artery
   8.0 m/s; the nonlinear tube steepens because c ∝ A^{−1/4} (Riemann invariant u + 4c).
9. **C09 Womersley's profile.** The corrected Bessel ODE $U''+U'/r-\dfrac{i\omega\rho}{\mu}U=-\dfrac G\mu$ (16.76, S6) →
   $u=\dfrac{Ga^2}{i\mu\alpha^2}\left(1-\dfrac{J_0(i^{3/2}\alpha r/a)}{J_0(i^{3/2}\alpha)}\right)e^{i\omega t}$ (16.82) (D13 ★★★); small α → breathing
   Poiseuille (16.87) (D14), large α → plug + Stokes layer (16.88) (D15, our wall-layer derivation); reversal starts at the wall.
10. **C10 How much flow, how late.** $Q\propto(1-F_2)$ (16.90), $F_2=\dfrac{2J_1(i^{3/2}\alpha)}{i^{3/2}\alpha J_0(i^{3/2}\alpha)}$ (16.91) (D16);
    $\lvert Q\rvert=\dfrac{\pi Ga^4}{\mu\alpha^2}\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}$ (16.99), $\tan\phi=X/Y$ (16.100) (D17): |Q|/|Q₀| = 0.986, 0.548, 0.105, 0.0186
    and lag 9.5°, 54.3°, 78.9°, 85.8° at α = 1, 3, 8, 20 — a damper at small α, a mass at large α; wall shear (16.92) is a
    stress with F₂ (S8) and leads the flow; the large-α asymptote (16.96) needs i^{3/2}/(2α) (S10).
11. **C11 Where waves meet viscosity.** Long-wave normal modes e^{i(kx − ωt)} (T1, opposite to C09) through the membrane
    equations to the quadratic (16.136) in X = k²c₀²/ω² (D18–D21, three ★★★); the pressure root (X → 1, chosen by
    continuation, T7) at α = 8: c/c₀ = 0.9541, k₂/k₁ = 0.0622, 0.677 of the amplitude kept per wavelength; both effects ∝ 1/α.
12. **C12 Murray's law.** Cost $\dfrac{8\mu L}{\pi a^4}Q^2+K\pi a^2L$ (16.138) → $a=\left[\dfrac{16\mu}{\pi^2K}\right]^{1/6}Q^{1/3}$ (16.140,
    K in the denominator, S15) (D22): pumping = ½ upkeep at the optimum; $a_0^3=a_1^3+a_2^3$ (16.144) (D23); angles by a weighted
    Fermat point (16.146)–(16.147) (D24 ★★★): symmetric fork arccos 2^{−1/3} = 37.47°, 70/30 split 25.71°/49.73°.
13. **C13 Echoes at a fork.** $Q=\pm Yp$ (16.151) with Y = A/(ρc); pressure continuity + flux conservation →
    $\mathrm R=\dfrac{Y_1-\sum Y_j}{Y_1+\sum Y_j}$, T = 1 + R (16.153) (D25); energy $\mathrm R^2+\dfrac{\sum Y_j}{Y_1}\mathrm T^2=1$ (ours, D26). A
    Murray fork with equal wave speeds has ΣY/Y₁ = 2^{1/3}: R = −0.115 (slightly open-end-like).
14. **C14 A bend makes two vortices.** Dean's series (16.177) fed the straight-pipe profile: ψ₀ from ∇⁴ψ₀ = 8r(1 − r²) sin α,
    w₁, flux coefficient 1541/50400 (D27 ★★★, sympy in view); K = 2κ², D = 4√2κ (T8); flux ratio 0.9814 at K = 576, axial
    maximum shifted 0.187a outward; the K⁴ coefficient is **6471982981/542442700800 = 0.0119312** with the corrected sign of
    (16.174) (S34; the printed sign gives 0.0121130, the book prints 0.0120).
15. **C15 Floppy tubes choke at S = 1.** The speed index S = u/c (16.195) plays M: Shapiro's
    $(1-S^2)\dfrac1\alpha\dfrac{d\alpha}{dx}=\dots$ (16.204)–(16.206) (D29, D30 ★★★; (16.201) corrected, S23); form the driver before
    dividing by 1 − S² (T10); tube law −P ≈ α^{−n} − 1 (16.188) **valid only for α ≤ 1**; Starling resistor plateau Q_choke =
    1.99899 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s at p₂ − p_e = −946.13 Pa (closed form x(α)); frictionless throat α_t = 0.35 ≤ (1 − n/2)^{1/n} =
    0.39685 → Q* = 47.646 cm³/s.
16. **C16 A plug riding on a ring.** τ = Δp r/(2L) (16.209) meets τ_y at $a_c=a\,\tau_y/\tau_w$ (16.210) (D31); annulus profile and
    plug speed (16.211)–(16.218) (D32); flux (16.220) (D33 ★★★, "considerable algebra" filled in as a polynomial in √ξ): no
    flow when a_c ≥ a (T21); minimum pressure drop 2Lτ_y/a (S26: the book's dyn/cm² figure is 1000× too small).
17. **C17 Water potential.** $\psi=p-RTc+\rho gz$ (16.221) (D34): sap climbs by moving down ψ under tension (p < 0, T22);
    (16.222) adds the sieve-plate drop in the phloem; S28 (the xylem Reynolds number needs water's ν), S30 (÷10⁶ for bar).

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only; each a rule, with the measured evidence)
- **(a) A fitted constitutive law carries its validity range in code.** (16.188) −P ≈ α^{−n} − 1 is a fit to the collapse
  branch; the first demo throat (α_t = 0.6) put the upstream tube at α = 1.50, where P is capped and c² falls as the tube
  inflates — non-physical, and every test passed. Fix: `ALPHA_MAX_COLLAPSE = 1.0`; scalars warn and return the
  extrapolated value, arrays get NaN + a warning, with a 1e-12 round-off allowance; the demo's validity becomes a
  computed criterion (H ≤ 0 ⇔ α_t ≤ (1 − n/2)^{1/n} = 0.39685, proved with sympy).
- **(b) Replace a bracketed root search by the closed form when one exists.** `brentq(…, 1e-6, 1e6)` for the critical
  area ratio raised for wide upstream sections (α* = 8.96e-7 at A₀/A₀,t = 25); α* = (ρQ²/(A₀²K_pn))^{1/(2−n)} needs no
  bracket. Same lesson in the Starling tube: integrating d(ln α) and the closed form x(α) gave Q_choke = 1.99899e-5 m³/s;
  the design's x-march overshot at 2.00290e-5.
- **(c) Two time conventions in one chapter** (e^{+iωt} for the rigid tube, e^{i(kx − ωt)} for the elastic tube): the
  Bessel argument changes from i^{3/2}α to e^{iπ/4}α; every docstring states its convention and the constants differ
  (`I32` vs `I12`).
- **(d) Bessel functions of a complex argument grow like e^{α/√2}**: evaluate ratios with `jve` (scaled) — `jv` itself
  overflows only near α ≈ 1000, but ratios of two huge numbers lose nothing when scaled; a from-scratch power series is
  accurate only for |z| ≲ 25 (terms grow to e^{|z|} before cancelling).
- **(e) One scalar must have one meaning across siblings**: `womersley_flow_rate(t, …, 100.0)` read the scalar as the
  n = 1 amplitude, `…_harmonics` as the mean G₀ — 245× apart. Now the harmonic version raises for any bare scalar.
- **(f) A sign slip in a printed PDE shows only at the order where the nonlinear term first matters**: (16.174)'s advection
  sign changes nothing up to c₂ = 1541/50400 and moves c₄ by 1.5 %. Found only because the verifier solved the Navier–Stokes
  form independently (Chebyshev–Fourier Newton) and compared at fourth order. Keep `printed_16_174=True` and pin both values.
- **(g) Evaluate only the branch you need in `np.where`**: the Windkessel's diastolic exponential overflowed for tiny RK
  inside the unused branch (RuntimeWarning under `-W error`); compute each piece on its own mask.
- **(h) "V3" means an asserted order**: nine functions labelled `converged` were independent-route agreements; four new
  order studies replaced them (quadrature 1.999/3.989, Windkessel CN 2.000, ODE-determinant series start 6.000, Dean
  spectral resolution geometric). A characteristic transport at C = 1 is exact — there is nothing to refine.
- **(i) A unit chain printed wrong poisons every downstream number**: 1 cP = 10⁻³ Pa s (S27, printed chain 100× off);
  dyn/cm² minimum pressure drop 1000× too small (S26); xylem Re needs water's ν (S28). Converters live in code, pint-checked.
- **(j) Sibling argument orders** (third chapter running): `vascular_resistance(mu, L, a)` vs `poiseuille_flow_rate(a, dp,
  L, mu)` — call by keyword (open, §0).
