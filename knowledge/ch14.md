## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
**Lift is circulation; the trailing edge chooses it; the wing tips make you pay for it.** C02–C07 are the first two
clauses, C08–C15 the third, C16 is the first clause used for thrust. Γ is **clockwise** positive in the whole chapter.
1. **C01 The wing in numbers.** $\Lambda\equiv s^2/A=s/\bar c$ (14.1), $\bar c=A/s$ (14.2); level flight asks for
   $C_L=\frac{W}{\frac12\rho U^2A}$ (D01). A is the **planform** area; a section's forces are per unit span.
2. **C02 The Kutta condition.** Ideal flow accepts any Γ; only one value puts the rear stagnation point on the sharp
   edge, $\left.\frac{dw}{d\zeta}\right\rvert_{\zeta=b}=0$. Found first by a root-finder on the flat plate, before any formula.
3. **C03 The starting vortex.** $\Gamma_{ABCD}=0=\Gamma_{\text{foil}}+\Gamma_{\text{starting}}$ from Kelvin's theorem
   $\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt}=0$ (5.8) (D02): the foil gets Γ only by shedding −Γ, so viscosity makes the lift as well as the drag.
4. **C04 The Zhukhovsky airfoil.** $z=\zeta+\frac{b^2}{\zeta}$ (14.3) on a circle through ζ = b; raising the centre bends
   the slit into the arc $x^2+(y+2b\cot2\beta)^2=(2b\csc2\beta)^2$ (D03 ★★★), moving it back fattens the nose:
   $c\simeq4b$, camber $\simeq\frac12\beta c$, $t_{\max}/c\simeq1.3e$ (14.8), where 1.3 is $3\sqrt3/4$ (D04 ★★★); the tail is
   a cusp (D05).
5. **C05 The flow round the airfoil.** $\frac{dw}{dz}=\frac{dw}{d\zeta}\frac{\zeta^2}{\zeta^2-b^2}$ (14.9) (D06, D07); the
   circulation is the same in both planes because $(dw/dz)\,dz=dw$ (D08); the velocity is not.
6. **C06 Lift.** $u_\theta=-2U\sin\theta-\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi a}$ (14.10) vanishing at the edge gives
   $\Gamma=4\pi Ua\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ (14.11) and $C_L\simeq2\pi(\alpha+\beta)$ (14.12) (D09); exactly
   $C_L=8\pi(a/c)\sin(\alpha+\beta)$ (ours), 9 % steeper than 2π at e = 0.1.
7. **C07 Surface speed and pressure (ours).** $C_p=1-\lvert dw/dz\rvert^2/U^2$ on the contour; the normal-force coefficient
   is the area of the pressure loop, $C_y=\oint C_p\,d(x/c)$ (D10); the steep rise behind the suction peak is what stalls.
8. **C08 Bound vortex and trailing filaments.** $d\Gamma_{\text{trail}}=-\frac{d\Gamma}{dy}\,dy$ (D11): a vortex cannot end
   in the fluid, so every drop in Γ leaves the wing as a streamwise filament.
9. **C09 Downwash.** $w(y_1)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-s/2}^{s/2}\frac{d\Gamma}{dy}\frac{dy}{(y_1-y)}$ (14.13) (D12), a
   principal value; one horseshoe gives Γ/(πs) at its centre; far behind the wing the downwash is 2w.
10. **C10 Induced drag.** $\alpha_e=\alpha-\frac wU$ (14.14); $L=\int\rho U\Gamma\,dy$, $D_i=\int\rho w\Gamma\,dy$ (14.15)
    (D13): the tilted stream tilts the force back; a drag that exists in ideal flow and equals the wake's kinetic energy
    per unit length.
11. **C11 The sine series.** $\Gamma=\sum\Gamma_n\sin(n\gamma)$ (14.18) with $y=-(s/2)\cos\gamma$ turns (14.13) into
    $w(y_1)=\frac{1}{2s}\sum n\Gamma_n\frac{\sin(n\gamma_1)}{\sin\gamma_1}$ (14.19, last member) through Glauert's integral
    (D14, D15 ★★★). **Slip #1**: the first two members are printed with the denominator in the wrong order.
12. **C12 The lifting-line equation.** $\frac K2Uc(\alpha+\beta)=\sum\big(1+\frac{nKc}{4s\sin\gamma}\big)\Gamma_n\sin(n\gamma)$
    (14.20) (D16): N stations, N linear equations, any planform and twist.
13. **C13 Forces from the coefficients.** $L=\frac{\pi s}{4}\rho U\Gamma_1$ (14.21), $D_i=\frac\pi8\rho\sum n\Gamma_n^2$
    (14.22) (D17, D18 ★★★): the lift needs only Γ₁; every other coefficient only adds drag.
14. **C14 The elliptic loading.** $\Gamma=\Gamma_1\sqrt{1-(2y/s)^2}$ (14.23), $w=\Gamma_1/2s$ (14.24),
    $D_i=\frac\pi8\rho\Gamma_1^2=\frac{2L^2}{\pi\rho U^2s^2}$ (14.25), $C_{D_i}=\frac{C_L^2}{\pi\Lambda}$ (14.26) (D19); slope
    of a finite wing $\frac{dC_L}{d\alpha}=\frac{K}{1+K/(\pi\Lambda)}$ (D20, ours).
15. **C15 Drag breakdown and the polar (ours).** $C_D=C_{D0}+\frac{C_L^2}{\pi\Lambda e_{\text{span}}}$; best glide
    $\big(\frac LD\big)_{\max}=\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\pi\Lambda e_{\text{span}}}{C_{D0}}}$ where the two parts are equal (D21).
16. **C16 Thrust from lift (ours).** $\mathbf U_r=\mathbf U-\mathbf V$; drive $=L\sin\beta_a-D\cos\beta_a$, zero at
    $\tan\beta_a=D/L$; closest course $\beta_a=\varepsilon_A+\varepsilon_H$ (D22): fish tail, bird and sail are one triangle.

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only; each a rule, with the measured evidence)
- **(a) Before overlaying a cited curve, ask which quantity it is a ratio of.** Wagner's function is circulatory lift
  over steady lift and starts at ½; the bound circulation obeys Kelvin's theorem and starts at 0. Design, script,
  docstring, verification report and the V5 test all put it on the circulation axis. **No code mutant could see it**: for
  a steady stream ρUΓ_bound/(ρUΓ_steady) and Γ_bound/Γ_steady are the same number (2e-16). Only a reader who asked
  "what does the conserved quantity say at t = 0⁺?" caught it.
- **(b) A numerical "time to reach x %" carries its time step.** The 90 % crossing is 6.37, 6.21, 6.12, 6.08, 6.06 chords
  at dt = 0.2 … 0.0125 c/U (first order, limit about 6.04). An assertion "within 0.1 of 6.2" let a wrong number live in
  three documents; assert the interpolated crossing at the stated step (±0.01) and print the step beside the number.
- **(c) Split a quadrature at every kink.** One 400-point Gauss rule across the mid-span kink of a tapered chord gave
  the area 2.2e-6 low, and C_L, C_Di and Λ inherited it; one rule per half-span is exact (2.0e-15). **The old test
  pinned the defect** ("differs by more than 1e-7") — a pinned number can pin a bug.
- **(d) Measure an order before writing it** (again; ch13 lesson h): the staircase of horseshoes converges at 1.5 with uniform
  panels and 2.0 with cosine panels (docstring said "first order"); the speed approaching a cusp converges as √d
  (docstring said 1e-4 at 1e-6 m; measured 2.2e-4); `section_geometry` is second order only when the trailing edge is a
  contour vertex.
- **(e) The first two members of a printed chain can be wrong while the last is right.** (14.19) prints
  $\cos\gamma_1-\cos\gamma$; with $y=-(s/2)\cos\gamma$ the difference is $y_1-y=(s/2)(\cos\gamma-\cos\gamma_1)$, and the
  printed middle member gives minus the last member (an independent principal-value quadrature, 2e-5). Test each member.
- **(f) Principal values**: `quad(weight="cauchy", wvar=)`, or subtract the singular part, or exclude a symmetric gap —
  three routes that must agree (1e-8, 2e-5); never sample the singular station; collocation stations
  γ_j = jπ/(N + 1) avoid the tips where sin γ = 0.
- **(g) 0/0 at a mapped singular point has a finite analytic limit** — code the limit
  ($U\frac ba\cos(\alpha+\beta)$ at the cusp), return `inf` when the Kutta circulation is not used, and test both.
- **(h) Choose the root of an inverse map by continuity, not by modulus**: the root nearest the circle's centre μ keeps
  `zhukhovsky_inverse` continuous across the slit under a cambered section; "larger |ζ|" does not.
- **(i) Lift is perpendicular to the stream, not to an axis.** With the stream inclined at α to x, `(Fx, Fy)` must be
  rotated; mutants "lift = F_y" and "Γ counterclockwise" were planted and killed.
- **(j) A survivor can be an order statement**: the sign of the closing value in the polygon rule of
  `surface_circulation` changes the result only at second order (the last segment at a cusp is O(h²)); the test now
  asserts the docstring's own tolerance.
- **(k) Scalar wrappers for parity rows** (`surface_point`, `edge_speed`, `map_point`) are part of the contract and are
  tested against the array functions; `np` is not visible inside a lambda in a `py:` row.
- **(l) K has no default anywhere** (per radian; four calls without it raise `TypeError`); empirical stall constants and
  Reynolds thresholds are required keywords and visibly ours.
