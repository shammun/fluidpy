## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
1. **C01 Lagrangian ↔ Eulerian (3.1)–(3.2).** A particle is a label (r_o, t_o) with a trajectory r(t; r_o, t_o); a
   field is F(x, t). The compatibility F[r(t; r_o, t_o), t] = F(x, t) at x = r *(3.2)* converts one into the other:
   invert x = Xe^{αt} for the label and u = αx drops out (D01).
2. **C02 Material derivative DF/Dt = ∂F/∂t + u·∇F (3.3)–(3.5).** The chain rule along a path with dr_i/dt = u_i
   (D02). Local change + advective change; the climate hook is thermal advection −u·∇T (a station warms while the air
   parcel does not). (3.6) is taught corrected: DF/Dt = ∂F/∂t + |u| ∂F/∂s (the book drops the F).
3. **C03 Streamlines dx/u = dy/v = dz/w (3.7).** Tangent to u at one frozen instant; the cross-product form
   u × ds = 0 (D03) survives where a component vanishes, the ratio form does not.
4. **C04 Path lines dr/dt = u(r, t) (3.8) and streak lines; Ex. 3.1.** A streak line is the family of path lines
   labelled by release time t_o. Ex. 3.1: streamline y = x tan ωt′, path and streak lines are mirror circles of
   radius ξ_o, all tangent at the port (D04, D05). The three coincide exactly when the flow is steady.
5. **C05 Galilean invariance (3.9).** With x = x′ + Ut + x′_o and u = U + u′, the U·∇′ terms cancel: the acceleration
   ∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = ∂u′/∂t′ + (u′·∇′)u′ is frame-free, its split into local and advective parts is not (D06). Steady
   vs unsteady depends on the observer (the towed cylinder).
6. **C06 Relative velocity du_i = (∂u_i/∂x_j) dx_j (3.10).** First-order Taylor of the neighbour's velocity (D07):
   all of a small element's motion lives in the nine numbers of G = S + ½R *(3.11)–(3.13)*, R = G − Gᵀ (no ½).
7. **C07 Linear strain rate.** (1/δx₁) D(δx₁)/Dt = ∂u₁/∂x₁; along any unit n it is n·S·n (D08; R drops out because
   n·R·n = 0).
8. **C08 Shear strain rate S₁₂ = ½ D(α + β)/Dt.** Half the closing rate of a right angle, 2n₁·S·n₂ in general (D09);
   a rigid motion U + Ω × x has S = 0 and ω = 2Ω (D10). **γ = du₁/dx₂ = 2S₁₂.**
9. **C09 Volumetric strain rate (1/δV) D(δV)/Dt = ∂u_i/∂x_i (3.14).** Three sides stretching, product rule (D11); the
   finite-time factor is det e^{Gt} = e^{t tr G} (Jacobi). Kinematic half of Ch. 4's continuity equation.
10. **C10 Spin = ½ vorticity (3.15)–(3.18).** The *average* turning rate of two perpendicular material lines is
    ½ω₃ (D12); in a parallel shear flow single lines turn at −γ sin²θ but every pair averages −γ/2 (D14); an observer
    rotating at Ω measures ω′_z = ω_z − 2Ω (D13, the relative-vs-absolute vorticity of Ch. 13). Irrotational
    ⇒ u = ∇φ (3.17) only in a simply connected region; circulation Γ = ∮u·ds = ∫ω·n dA (3.18).
11. **C11 du = S·dx + ½ω × dx (3.19).** Deformation plus a rigid rotation at ω/2 (D15; one ε index swap flips the
    sign). The reason only S can enter the stress law of Ch. 4.
12. **C12 Principal strain axes (3.20)–(3.21).** In S's eigenframe each axis stretches in proportion to its own length,
    so a small sphere becomes an ellipsoid (D16). First order in dt only: for finite t in a shear flow the axes drift
    from 45° (43.57° at γt = 0.1 exactly; the ellipse turns at ≈ γ/4, not γ/2).
13. **C13 Polar vorticity ω_z = (1/r)∂(ru_θ)/∂r − (1/r)∂u_r/∂θ (3.23).** Circulation round a small polar sector ÷ area
    (D17). Solid body u_θ = ω₀r *(3.22)* → 2ω₀; line vortex u_θ = B/r *(3.25)* → 0 off the axis with Γ = 2πB *(3.26)*
    and a δ core *(3.27)*: going round is not spinning (paddle wheels orbit without turning).
14. **C14 Rankine (3.28) and Gaussian (3.29) vortices.** A uniform core joined to an irrotational outside peaks at σ
    (a kink, D18); the Gaussian's u_θ follows from ω_z by Stokes, Γ(r) = Γ(1 − e^{−r²/σ²}) (D19), and peaks where
    1 + 2x = eˣ, x = r²/σ² = 1.25643, r = 1.1209σ (D20). Lamb–Oseen is σ² = 4νt (Ch. 5/8).
15. **C15 Reynolds transport theorem (3.30)–(3.35).** Leibniz in 1-D: interior change + ḃF(b) − ȧF(a) (D21). In 3-D,
    split V*(t + Δt) into V*(t) plus a **signed** swept sliver (b·n Δt dA); the Δt·ΔV term is O(Δt²) and drops (D22
    ★★★): d/dt ∫_{V*} F dV = ∫_{V*} ∂F/∂t dV + ∫_{A*} F b·n dA. With b = u it returns (3.14) (D23) and
    (3.5) plus the hidden F∇·u (D24).

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls)
- **Letter reuse is the chapter's main trap: pin each meaning with a test.** γ = du₁/dx₂ = 2S₁₂ (ch03 §3.5) vs ch02
  Ex. 2.4's Γ ≡ S₁₂ vs `velocity_gradient_preset("simple_shear", Gamma)` where Γ = du₁/dx₂ vs Γ = circulation
  (`core/vortices.py`). The "shear rate γ instead of S₁₂" variant fails 3 tests; the preset docstring spells the split out
  (review Should-fix 3).
- **Book typos found by dimensions, implemented corrected and tested both ways**: (3.6) as printed is 1/s, not K/s — the
  `…_V2_dimensions_expose_book_typo` test proves the printed form is wrong; Ex. 3.2's "b = 0 on the base" is only
  b·n = 0 (b ≠ 0 there, tested); the "[?]" in Ex. 3.2's integrand is z (the integral without z differs); (3.19) follows
  from (3.15), not (3.14); Fig. 3.16's C is B.
- **Signed swept volume.** The RTT sliver is b·n Δt dA with its sign; `|b·n|` fails 5 tests. Retreating walls subtract.
- **r* = σ√x*, not σx*.** The Gaussian maximum is in x = r²/σ²; the slip 1.2564σ fails 3 tests. Two independent routes
  (brentq on 1 + 2x − eˣ in (0.5, 3); Lambert W₋₁) agree to 1e-13; `gaussian_vortex` uses `-expm1(-x)/r` (no
  cancellation at r = 1e-9σ).
- **Rotating ≠ Galilean frame.** ω′ = ω − 2Ω (not ω − Ω; that variant fails 2 tests); a Galilean shift leaves the
  acceleration unchanged but the rotating one does not (Coriolis, Ch. 4 §4.7).
- **Unit-mixing defaults**: `material_derivative_terms` once reused the length step h [m] as the time step [s]
  (review Should-fix 5) — a time step gets its own documented default `ht`.
- **Vectorise over points, then trace over the right axes**: `volumetric_strain_rate` failed on (d, d, N) gradients
  (`float(np.trace(G))`); trace over axes 0, 1 and wrap with `as_scalar_if_0d`.
- **Documentation must describe negative inputs**: `parallel_shear_kinematics(γ < 0)` returns the stretching axis first
  (diag(|γ|/2, −|γ|/2), −45°), not diag(γ/2, −γ/2) — documented.
- **Closed loops wrap**: `scripts/ch03_fig3_18_rtt.py` dropped the last boundary segment (gap in the swept band) — index
  modulo n.
- **sympy `Subs`/`Derivative` objects do not simplify to 0** under a change of frame (D06 check printed
  `-Subs(Derivative(f(...)))+Subs(...)`): substitute generic polynomials for the unknown functions before `simplify`
  (verification O1).
- **Check numbers carry their true digits**: "u_θ(5) = 0.19999998" was wrong (it is 0.19999999999722); write "0.2 to 11
  digits" (O2). An explainer's live number must use the explainer's own geometry (O3: ellipse a = 2, b = 1 vs 1, 0.6).
- **Form cross-checks are not benchmarks**: Wikipedia's Rankine, Lamb–Oseen, RTT and Leibniz forms were relabelled V1
  (form cross-check); the only V5 is SWIRL's published α = 1.256.
- **Labels follow the evidence actually asserted**: `pathline` and `rtt_ellipse_2d` said "converged" without a measured
  order → relabelled `analytic`.
- **Book-rounded constants stay out of the public tests** (1.12091 → the Lambert-W value; review Should-fix 8).
- Measured rates converge at order 1 in dt (tracked segments) while stencils and quadratures converge at order 2: say
  which, and fit the slope (stretch 1.002; RTT FD 1.998; one-sided quotient 1.008).

## 5. Conventions used (see `knowledge/notation.md`)
- Dimensional SI; no reference scales (E6 draws in r/σ with real-vortex modes in metres).
- **Velocity field callables `u(x, t)`**, x of shape (d,) or (d, N) with coordinates on axis 0 (ch02 integral routines
  take `fn(X, Y[, Z])`; adapt with `as_coord_field`).
- **G[i, j] = ∂u_i/∂x_j; S = ½(G + Gᵀ) (3.12); R = G − Gᵀ (3.13, no ½); ω = vector of R = ∇×u (3.15); the element
  spins at ½ω; ω₃ = R₂₁ in 2-D.** G = S + ½R.
- Angles and rotations counterclockwise positive: in the shear u = (γx₂, 0), ω₃ = −γ (clockwise), a line at θ turns at
  −γ sin²θ, pairs average −γ/2.
- **γ = du₁/dx₂ = 2S₁₂** (ch03); ch02 Ex. 2.4's Γ = S₁₂; **Γ = circulation [m²/s]** in ch03 (`core/vortices.py`).
- **Galilean frame (3.9)**: O′ moves at constant U with parallel axes, x = x′ + Ut + x′_o, u′ = u − U. Cylinder: body
  frame = fluid at infinity moves at +U e_x (steady); fluid frame = cylinder moves at −U e_x (unsteady); E3's
  `U_frame` ∈ [0, U] interpolates (0 = fluid, U = body). **Primes mean a translating frame here, not ch02's rotated
  axes.**
- **Rotating frame**: u = Ω × x + u′ at the instant the frames coincide; ω′ = ω − 2Ω.
- Coordinates: plane polar (r, θ from +x); cylindrical (R, φ, z) with capital R; spherical (r, θ **from +z**, φ azimuth);
  unit vectors returned as rows E[k] (E = Cᵀ of ch02); φ = 0 on the axis.
- **RTT**: V*(t), A*(t), outward n, b = surface velocity (need not follow the fluid; only b·n matters), swept volume
  **signed**; Leibniz `lower` term is the one subtracted.
- Ex. 3.1 names kept apart: t′ drawing instant, t_o release time, ξ_o amplitude, ω the oscillation's angular frequency
  (**not vorticity** — the flow is spatially uniform, its vorticity is 0).
- Line-vortex strength B (book's Fig. 3.16 writes C; ch02 called it K).
- Changes vs earlier chapters: the ch02 linear-flow helpers moved to `core.kinematics` (ch02 re-exports, its 92 tests
  unchanged); Γ gained a third and fourth meaning; primes changed meaning (rotated → translating frame); ω also means an
  angular frequency in Ex. 3.1; σ = core radius (ch01: surface tension); φ = azimuth and velocity potential.
