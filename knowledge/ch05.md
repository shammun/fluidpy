## 1. What clicked — the teaching spine (our words, with equation numbers)
The chapter follows vorticity through its life: **where it lives (tubes), what it does to pressure, when it is
conserved (Kelvin, Helmholtz), how it changes (the vorticity equation, then rotation and density), how it makes
velocity (Biot–Savart), and how vortices move each other (pairs, images, sheets).**
1. **C01 Tubes cannot end (5.4).** Vortex lines are tangent to ω, dx/ω_x = dy/ω_y = dz/ω_z *(5.3)*; a tube's strength
   Γ = ∮u·dx = ∫ω·n dA. Gauss on a piece of tube with ∇·ω = 0: ∫∇·ω dV = −Γ_lower + Γ_upper = 0 *(5.4)* (D01) — the same
   strength at every cross-section, so a thinning tube spins faster (1 cm² → 0.25 cm²: 100 → 400 s⁻¹) and a tube can end
   only on a wall or close on itself. Kinematic: true in viscous flow too. A "broken" field (∇·ω ≠ 0) fails the budget by
   exactly ∫∇·ω dV.
2. **C02 Pressure in the two basic vortices (5.6), (5.7).** Solid body u_θ = ωr/2 *(5.1)* — **ω is the vorticity, the tank
   turns at ω/2** — has S = 0, so Euler; the turning e_θ gives −u_θ²/r = −(1/ρ)∂p/∂r *(5.5a)*, hydrostatics *(5.5b)*,
   partial integration leaves f(z): p − p_o = ⅛ρω²r² − ρgz *(5.6)* (D02), paraboloid isobars (a bowl). The line vortex
   u_θ = Γ/2πr *(5.2)* gives the funnel p − p_∞ = −ρΓ²/8π²r² − ρgz *(5.7)*; it has σ_rθ = −μΓ/πr² ≠ 0 but **zero net viscous
   force** (D03, three routes; the r² metric factor is the trap). Irrotational means no net viscous force, not no stress;
   the Rankine composite gives the tornado a finite bottom; the rotating cylinder carries a torque −2μΓ to infinity.
3. **C03 Kelvin's theorem (5.8).** Differentiate Γ of a *material* loop with fixed particle labels: DΓ/Dt =
   ∮(Du_i/Dt)dx_i + ∮u_i D(dx_i)/Dt *(5.9)*, the second term ∮d(½u²) = 0 (D04); substitute momentum *(5.10)*: pressure
   drops only if ρ = ρ(p) (**"single-valued" is not enough** — a baroclinic field gives ∮dp/ρ = −∫(∇ρ×∇p/ρ²)·n dA),
   gravity drops if conservative, leaving DΓ/Dt = ∮(1/ρ)∂σ_ij/∂x_j dx_i *(5.11)*, zero when inviscid (D05). Four
   restrictions (inviscid, barotropic, conservative, inertial), three sources (nonconservative forces, baroclinicity,
   viscosity); a 16-row decision table names the surviving term. A cellular loop stretched ×6.7 keeps Γ = 1.155130 to
   8e-13; a Lamb–Oseen circle decays exactly as Γ₀(1 − e^{−r²/4νt}).
4. **C04 The baroclinic pressure torque (Fig. 5.6).** On a small disc the pressure force passes through the geometric
   centre while the centre of mass sits R²∇ρ/4ρ₀ toward the heavy side; torque ÷ I_G = ½πρ₀R⁴, doubled, is exactly
   (1/ρ²)∇ρ × ∇p — the baroclinic term *(5.28)*, derived here from a picture (D06). Lock exchange: 2(ρ₂ − ρ₁)g/((ρ₂ + ρ₁)δ)
   = 2.42 s⁻² for fresh/salt water over δ = 0.1 m (D07); its δ-independent tank-integrated rate leads to the vortex sheet.
5. **C05 Helmholtz's theorems.** Kelvin on every surface lying on a tube wall (zero edge circulation stays zero) keeps
   the wall a wall: vortex lines move with the fluid; with (5.4) and (5.8) the other three follow (D08). After C06 the
   field route: ω and a material element obey the same linear equation when ν = 0 (frozen-in angle < 1e-8).
6. **C06 The vorticity equation (5.13).** Curl of incompressible NS kills pressure and gravity *(5.12)*; the Lamb identity
   and ∇×(ω×u) = (u·∇)ω − (ω·∇)u (with ∇·u = ∇·ω = 0) give Dω/Dt = (ω·∇)u + ν∇²ω *(5.13)* (D09 ★★★): carried,
   stretched/tilted, diffused — **pressure cannot touch vorticity in a uniform-density fluid**. Checked on Lamb–Oseen,
   Taylor–Green, Burgers, Hill, ABC; the diffusing sheet ω = γ/(2√(πνt)) e^{−y²/4νt} keeps ∫ω dy = γ.
7. **C07 Biot–Savart (5.16).** ∇×ω = −∇²u is a Poisson equation; its Green's function gives u = **+**(1/4π)∫(∇′×ω)/|x−x′| d³x′
   — **the book prints −1/(4π) in (5.14)** (D10 ★★★) and makes a compensating slip in the integrand rewrite, so (5.16) is
   right; product rule, ∇′(1/r) = +(x−x′)/r³, Gauss in curl form *(5.15)* and a tube-aligned V′ give
   u = (1/4π)∫ω(x′) × (x − x′)/|x − x′|³ d³x′ *(5.16)* (D11 ★★★). Vorticity here decides velocity everywhere.
8. **C08 The filament law (5.17).** Far from a thin tube the kernel is frozen across the core (relative variation
   ≈ 2a/|x − x′|: 20 % at 10 radii, 2 % at 100), ∫ω d²x′ = Γ (D12): du = (Γdl/4π)e_ω × (x − x′)/|x − x′|³ *(5.17)*. A
   segment gives (Γ/4πd)(cos θ_a − cos θ_b), the infinite line Γ/2πd = (5.2) (D13); square loop 2√2Γ/πL at its centre.
9. **C09 Rotating, baroclinic vorticity equation (5.30).** Rotating NS in Lamb form *(5.25)* (D14), then its curl in index
   notation (D15 ★★★; the book silently drops u_{j,j}(ω_n + 2Ω_n), killed by ∇·u = 0):
   Dω/Dt = (ω + 2Ω)·∇u + (1/ρ²)∇ρ × ∇p + ν∇²ω *(5.30)* — absolute vorticity is stretched and tilted, density crossing
   pressure makes vorticity, viscosity diffuses it; Ω = 0 and ρ = ρ(p) give (5.13) back. A column with ∂w/∂z = 1e-5 s⁻¹
   gains 2Ω∂w/∂z = 1.458×10⁻⁹ s⁻².
10. **C10 Stretching and tilting (5.32).** Natural coordinates on a vortex line: (ω·∇)u = ω∂u/∂s *(5.31)* (D16);
    Dω_s/Dt = ω∂u_s/∂s (stretching), Dω_n/Dt = ω∂u_n/∂s, Dω_m/Dt = ω∂u_m/∂s (tilting) *(5.32)*, none in 2-D (D17) — the
    skater. Inviscid: a tube stretched ×2 doubles ω; Burgers' vortex holds stretching against diffusion in a core
    √(4ν/α) = 2 mm for α = 1 s⁻¹, ν = 1e-6 (D18). Axial strain α = 1 s⁻¹ multiplies ω by e² = 7.389 in 2 s.
11. **C11 Absolute circulation (5.33) and the fluid column.** The Coriolis loop integral is −2Ω·dA_vec/dt (D19), so
    DΓ_a/Dt = 0 with Γ_a = Γ + 2∫Ω·n dA *(5.33)*; round a thin column of fixed mass Ah: (ω_z + 2Ω)/h = const (D20) —
    stretched columns spin cyclonically (1000 → 1100 m at f = 1e-4: ζ = +1e-5 s⁻¹), squashed ones anticyclonically; a
    500-km ring at rest moved from 30° N to 60° N gets Γ = −4.19×10⁷ m²/s. **The seed of potential vorticity (Ch. 13).**
12. **C12 Point vortices move each other.** A line vortex does not move itself; each is carried by the others:
    dx_k/dt = Σ_{j≠k}(Γ_j/2π) e_z × (x_k − x_j)/|x_k − x_j|². Like vortices orbit the centre of vorticity
    h₁ = Γ₂h/(Γ₁ + Γ₂) at (Γ₁ + Γ₂)/2πh² (period 19.74 s for Γ = 1 m²/s, h = 1 m); an opposite pair marches at Γ/2πh (D21).
    Fig. 5.11's G is the centre of vorticity, **not** a stagnation point (fluid there moves at −1.70 m/s for Γ₂ = 3Γ₁).
13. **C13 Images.** A wall is an opposite mirror vortex at 2h: the wall is a streamline and the vortex slides along it at
    Γ/4πh (D22); circle (inverse point) and channel ((Γ/4H)cot(πh/H)) images; a ring toward a wall widens and slows, two
    coaxial rings leap-frog (thin-core model; stops honestly where the gap < 3 core radii).
14. **C14 The vortex sheet.** A row of line vortices makes a velocity jump; the circulation of a thin dn × ds box gives
    dΓ = (u₂ − u₁)ds (D23): **strength = jump in tangential velocity**, counterclockwise positive with u₁ above, u₂ below
    (the Fig. 5.16 caption prints u₁ − u₂, the clockwise count). N filaments converge to the jump as 1/N; left alone the
    sheet rolls up (Kelvin–Helmholtz, Ch. 11).

## 4. Mathematics → Python lessons (new pitfalls only)
- **Probe a function exactly at its piecewise boundaries.** F1: at r = a (Rankine core edge, cylinder wall) the net
  viscous force used a central difference that straddled the kink of u_θ: half the torque and a "force" growing as 1/h
  under a status saying "no net viscous force". Fix: differentiate the fluid-side profile, report the stress jump as a
  line force (`edge_line_force` = −μΓ/πa²) and NaN for the pointwise force. Tests now evaluate at a, 0.999a, 1.001a.
- **Check a field against its own stream function.** F2: Hill's exterior u_R had the wrong sign (fluid crossed the
  sphere at ±0.154 m/s); the inside and ψ were right. A test comparing u with −R⁻¹∂ψ/∂z caught it; the normal
  velocity on r = a⁺ is now ≤ 4e-7.
- **A test field can hide a transpose.** The planetary-term variant 2Gᵀ·Ω passed the rotating-column test because that
  G is symmetric in its z row and column; a tilting field u = (sz, 0, 0) and a generic-field comparison were added.
  Every convention test needs a field where the wrong variant gives a different number.
- **scipy's elliptic integrals take the parameter m = k², not the modulus k** (`ellipk(m)`, `ellipe(m)`): passing k
  misses ring velocities by > 5 % and is invisible without a polygon cross-check (order 1.997).
- **Book sign slips can cancel.** (5.14) prints −1/(4π) and the integrand rewrite flips another sign, so (5.16) is
  right. Code the corrected (5.14) (`sign=+1`), keep the printed sign as an option, and test that it reverses the swirl.
- **Keep what the book drops when checking it symbolically**: the D15 sympy check carries u_{j,j}(ω_n + 2Ω_n) for a

## 5. Conventions used (see `knowledge/notation.md`)
- Dimensional SI; z up; g = 9.81 (`core.thermo.G_BOOK`, the chapter module's default); counterclockwise-positive plane
  vorticity, circulation and point-vortex strength; latitudes in degrees only at `*_deg` interfaces.
- **ω is the vorticity**: in (5.1) u_θ = ωr/2 the tank turns at Ω = ω/2 (ch03's `solid_body_rotation(r, omega0)` takes
  the rate; `solid_body_from_vorticity` converts). Fig. 5.2's "2ω" label is a slip.
- **Γ is always a circulation [m²/s]** (`Gamma`); the sheet strength is γ [m/s] (`gamma`), γ = u₂ − u₁ = u_below −
  u_above (counterclockwise circuit); `convention="caption"` gives the caption's u₁ − u₂. Γ_a = Γ + 2∫Ω·n dA.
- **Baroclinic sense**: (∇ρ × ∇p)_z in that order; E4 and the notebook use ∇p = (0, −ρ₀g) and ∇ρ = |∇ρ|(sin θ, −cos θ):
  θ = 0 stable (no torque), 0 < θ < 180° clockwise (−0.0981 s⁻² at 90° for |∇ρ| = 10 kg/m⁴), θ = 270° counterclockwise;
  lock exchange with heavy fluid on the left: +2.42 s⁻², counterclockwise.
- **Natural coordinates**: e_s along ω, **e_n away from the centre of curvature = −(Frenet N)** (Fig. 5.9), e_m = e_s × e_n.
- (5.14) with +1/(4π); ∇′(1/|x − x′|) = +(x − x′)/|x − x′|³ (derivative with respect to the source point).
- (5.30)'s "Boussinesq" keeps the full 1/ρ in the pressure term (to leading order ∇ρ × ∇p/ρ² ≈ ∇ρ′ × g/ρ₀).
- Burgers: the book's α (u_z = αz, u_R = −½αR) is twice Wikipedia's; core √(4ν/α).
- Elliptic integrals with the parameter m = k²; ring cores: uniform ¼, hollow ½, Gaussian 0.558 with a = √(4νt).
- **Changes vs earlier chapters**: ω (ch03 `omega0` = rotation rate; ch02/03 ω = vorticity; Ex. 3.1 frequency) — here
  vorticity, with Ω = ω/2 for solid body; **Γ now has five meanings** (lapse rate ch01, preset rate ch02/03, Ex. 2.4's
  S₁₂, circulation ch03 →, and the book's per-length sheet strength, which we write γ); σ = viscous stress (ch03 core
  radius is `sigma_core` here); ε = Levi-Civita in (5.18)–(5.29) and dissipation in N08; ζ = relative vorticity (ch01
  displacement, ch04 cap height); h = column height, vortex spacing, wall distance; α = strain rate (ch01 thermal
  expansion); τ = torsion of a vortex line (stress elsewhere); m = elliptic parameter (mass elsewhere); H = channel
  width and point-vortex energy; s = arc length and a shear rate in the strain presets; θ = baroclinic tilt and segment
  end angles.
