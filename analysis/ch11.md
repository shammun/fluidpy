## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words; what is new vs restated from earlier chapters)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the five objectives):**
1. Give the mathematical machinery of *temporal* linear instability: put a small wave-like disturbance on a known steady flow,
   linearise, and ask whether its amplitude grows in time (an eigenvalue problem for a growth rate σ or a complex wave speed c).
2. Apply it to confined and unconfined flows, one mechanism at a time: shear (Kelvin–Helmholtz), buoyancy (Rayleigh–Bénard),
   two diffusivities (double diffusion, salt fingers), rotation/centrifugal (Taylor–Couette), shear against stratification
   (Richardson number, Howard's semicircle).
3. Present the classical theorems for parallel shear flows: Squire (2-D disturbances suffice), the Orr–Sommerfeld and Rayleigh
   equations, Rayleigh's inflection-point and Fjørtoft's criteria, critical layers.
4. Report the viscous results (neutral curves, critical Reynolds numbers of Table 11.1, Tollmien–Schlichting waves and their
   experimental confirmation) and explain how viscosity can *destabilise* through the disturbance-energy budget.
5. Discuss what linear theory cannot do — nonlinear saturation, transition scenarios — and show how a three-variable model of
   convection (Lorenz) produces deterministic chaos; routes to chaos (period doubling with Feigenbaum's ratio, quasi-periodicity).

**Outline with equation ranges:** 11.1 Introduction (Fig. 11.1; no equations) · 11.2 Normal modes (11.1) · 11.3 Kelvin–Helmholtz
(11.2)–(11.20), mixing energy · 11.4 Bénard (11.21)–(11.44) · 11.5 Double diffusion (11.45)–(11.46) · 11.6 Taylor (11.47)–(11.54)
· 11.7 Stratified parallel flows (11.55)–(11.72) · 11.8 Squire and Orr–Sommerfeld (11.73)–(11.80) · 11.9 Inviscid parallel flows
(11.81)–(11.87) · 11.10 Viscous results (11.88), Table 11.1 · 11.11 TS waves (no numbered equation; Tollmien profile) · 11.12
Nonlinear comments · 11.13 Transition · 11.14 Deterministic chaos (11.89)–(11.91) · Exercises (11.92)–(11.96).

**New vs restated.** Restated from earlier chapters: the Boussinesq set (ch04), N² (ch01/ch07), velocity potentials and the
linearised interface conditions (ch06/ch07 §7.7, including the static-interface dispersion relation (11.19) = ch07 (7.95)),
vortex sheets and roll-up (ch05), circular Couette flow and its Rayleigh criterion (ch08), Blasius/Falkner–Skan and the
inflection rule (ch09), perturb → linearise → eigenvalues (ch09 Kármán street, P214), Fourier modes and growth factors
(ch10 von Neumann). **New:** the normal-mode *stability* vocabulary (σ, c, marginal vs neutral, exchange of stabilities,
overstability), every named instability and number of the chapter (Ra, Rs, Ta, gradient-Ri criterion, Re_c), the Orr–
Sommerfeld/Rayleigh/Taylor–Goldstein equations and the integral-identity proof technique (multiply by the conjugate, integrate
by parts, take real and imaginary parts), Squire's transformation, critical layers, the disturbance-energy budget, phase space,
attractors, bifurcations, the Lorenz system and Feigenbaum universality. Climate hooks the curator may use: Ri < ¼ and KH
billows in the atmosphere and ocean thermocline, salt fingers and thermohaline staircases, Rayleigh–Bénard ↔ atmospheric
convection, the rotating annulus → baroclinic instability (ch13), Lorenz's weather-predictability motivation.

## 9. Risks, conventions, ambiguities, suspected typos

**Printed slips (named S1–S12; in the notebook call them "slip #1 … #12" so they cannot collide with recap IDs R01…)**
- **S1 (p. 489, derivative line of the even solution):** the third term of (d²/dz² − K²)²W is printed "B(q*² − K²)² cosh q*z"; it
  must be **C**(q*² − K²)² cosh q*z (the matrix below it is right). Evidence: the book's own determinant with C gives
  Ra_c = 1707.7618 at K = 3.1163 (analyst), identical to Chebyshev.
- **S2 (p. 490):** "W = A sin(nπz)" with z ∈ [−½, ½] satisfies W(±½) = 0 only for even n; the family is sin nπ(z + ½)
  (n = 1: cos πz — the form Lorenz's (11.90) actually uses). (11.44) is unaffected (the eigenvalue depends on n² only).
- **S3 (p. 490):** dRa/dK² printed 3(π² + K²)²/K² − **3**(π² + K²)³/K⁴; the second term has no factor 3 (quotient rule). With the
  printed form the root equation reduces to K² = π² + K² (no solution); the correct form gives K_c² = π²/2 as the book states.
- **S4 ((11.55), second equation):** "−(1/ρ₀)∂p/∂x" in the w-equation must be ∂p/∂z (the following (11.57) uses ∂p/∂z).
- **S5 (p. 480):** "(11.19) is the dispersion relation for interface waves … see (7.96)" — (7.96) is the interface-wave *energy*;
  the dispersion relation is ch07 **(7.95)** (checked on the ch07 page).
- **S6 (p. 520, Tollmien profile):** the middle branch as printed, 1 − b[1 − (y/δ)²], is discontinuous with the inner linear branch (it is almost 0 at the breakpoint while the inner branch is about 0.29); **1 − b(1 − y/δ)²** (square outside the bracket) matches the inner branch in value and slope to the book's rounding (< 0.5 %) and reaches 1 with zero slope at y = δ. Implement the corrected form, keep `printed=True` (the coefficients a, b and the breakpoint are book values, private).
- **S7 ((11.93), Exercise 11.9):** the operator is printed squared, (d²/dR² − k²)²û_φ = −Ta k²û_R; (11.51) at σ = 0 gives the first
  power. With the square the system is 8th order and Ta_c changes.
- **S8 (p. 507):** "∫[U_min − U][U_max − U]dz ≤ 0 … can be recast as ∫[…]Q dz ≤ 0" — the weight Q ≥ 0 must be inside from the
  start (the unweighted integral is not what is used); also "(U_min − U) < 0" should be ≤.
- **S9 (stream-function signs, not an error):** §11.7 uses u = ∂ψ/∂z, w = −∂ψ/∂x; §11.8 uses u = ∂ψ/∂y, v = −∂ψ/∂x; §11.14 uses
  u = −∂ψ/∂z, w = ∂ψ/∂x. State the convention in every function docstring.
- **S10 (sign of Γ, convention change):** (11.21) defines **Γ = −dT̄/dz** (positive when heated from below) and (11.24) Γ ≡ ΔT/d;
  ch01/ch04 code and the project's lapse-rate rule use **Kundu Γ ≡ dT/dz** (lapse rate). Never reuse `core.stratification` Γ here; name
  the input `dT` (= T_bottom − T_top) or `dTdz` explicitly and show both conventions in the notebook. §11.5 flips again: there
  "Ra ≡ gαd⁴(dT̄/dz)/(νκ)" is *negative* for heating from below (the book says so), so (11.45) carries −Ra.
- **S11 (p. 506):** the divergence form is printed d/dz[(U − c)²dF/dz] − k²(U − c)F + N²F = 0; expanding the preceding
  line (U − c)[(U − c)F″ + 2U′F′ − k²(U − c)F] + N²F = 0 gives **−k²(U − c)²F** (the next line's integral, with
  (U − c)²∣F∣², confirms it). Checked on the page image; the sympy test decides.
- **S12 ((11.47) and (11.50), continuity):** printed ∂(Rũ_R)/∂R + ∂ũ_z/∂z = 0 mixes units (velocity vs 1/s); the
  axisymmetric continuity equation is (1/R)∂(Rũ_R)/∂R + ∂ũ_z/∂z = 0. Harmless in the narrow-gap limit only after
  rescaling; implement the correct form.
- **Minor text-extraction traps (page is right):** the imaginary part of (11.65) has "= −c_i∫…" on the page (extraction drops the
  minus); v̂ = −ikφ in Ex. 11.12 (extraction drops the minus); the ± signs of (11.18) and (11.20) are on the page.

**Conventions and silent switches**
- **Three different non-dimensionalisations**: §11.4 lengths by d, time by d²/κ, w *left dimensional* until W ≡ (Γd²/κ)ŵ; §11.6
  lengths by the gap d, x ∈ [0, 1], σ by d²/ν (inferred, not stated), and "d/dR" in (11.51) means d/dx; §11.8 L (half-width for
  Poiseuille, δ* for Blasius, L of tanh/sech²), U₀ (centreline/free-stream/asymptotic), time L/U₀, pressure ρU₀². Each ST/ch11
  function states its scales; Re definitions differ per flow (Table 11.1's Poiseuille uses the half-width; pipe uses U_max d).
- **Two eigenvalue conventions**: σ (growth rate, e^{σt}) in §11.4, §11.6; c (complex phase speed, e^{ik(x−ct)}) in §11.3, §11.7–11.9;
  σ = −ikc. Code returns both where meaningful (`sigma_from_c`).
- **K vs k vs κ**: K = horizontal wavenumber magnitude (Bénard, non-dimensional), k = streamwise/axial wavenumber, κ = thermal
  diffusivity, κ_s salt diffusivity; k also appears as a wave number in Lorenz's b. **α overloaded**: thermal expansion (§11.4–11.5)
  and α = Ω₂/Ω₁ − 1 (§11.6); code uses `alpha` (thermal) and `mu` (= Ω₂/Ω₁). **β**: haline coefficient here (not ch10's FTCS β).
  **σ**: growth rate here vs surface tension (Ex. 11.1, ch01) vs vortex core (ch03): code `sigma` = growth rate, `surface_tension`.
  **Γ**: circulation 2πrU_θ in §11.6 vs temperature gradient in §11.4 (S10). **U₁**: upper-stream speed (§11.3) vs U at the inflection
  point (§11.9, Fjørtoft) — code `U_I`. **r**: Lorenz reduced Rayleigh number vs radius. **R**: nonlinearity parameter (§11.14) vs
  radius (§11.6). **φ**: velocity potential (§11.3), Rayleigh/TG transformed variable (§11.7), OS amplitude (§11.8) — the book warns.
  **Pr** appears in the Lorenz system as the "σ" of the usual literature.
- **Hidden assumptions to state**: Howard's semicircle uses N² ≥ 0; Miles–Howard's proof divides by U − c (needs c_i ≠ 0); exchange
  of stabilities proven for Bénard (Ra > 0) and for co-rotating Taylor–Couette only; Squire's theorem is for parallel flows (not
  for stratified or rotating ones in general — §11.7 *assumes* it); the Orr–Sommerfeld "parallel flow" is an approximation for
  boundary layers (non-parallel corrections §11.11); the double-diffusive marginal analysis assumes σ = 0 (valid for fingers, not
  for the oscillatory diffusive regime); Bénard's hexagons were Marangoni (surface-tension) convection.
- **Book vs benchmark rounding (show both, book's first per the ch09 rule, book's number only where it is also a standard)**:
  Poiseuille (book value, private) vs 5772.22; Blasius 520 (Jordinson) vs 519.2 (Thomas); rigid–free (book value, private) vs
  1100.65; (11.54) is itself approximate (0.77 % high at μ = 0).
- **Numerical risks**: spurious eigenvalues of Chebyshev OS (filter |c| < c_max *and* N-convergence; the TS mode is a slow, weakly
  unstable wall mode easily lost by an over-tight filter); ill-conditioning for N > 150 (use the φ-formulation, not primitive
  variables, except in the Squire test); unbounded domains (tanh, sech², Blasius) need a mapping or a large box with a convergence
  check in y_max; the Rayleigh equation's critical-layer singularity makes neutral modes (c_i → 0) converge slowly — compute
  growth rates at small positive c_i and extrapolate, never assert digits on a neutral Rayleigh mode except the analytic ones;
  the determinant route (#62) is purely imaginary (use Im) and has spurious sign changes near Ra = K⁴ (bracket above);
  Taylor–Goldstein with N² > 0 is a quadratic eigenproblem — the companion linearisation doubles the spurious modes; Lorenz
  trajectories: assert invariants and statistics, never a pointwise value beyond the predictability time (≈ 20 time units);
