## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the chapter objectives):**
1. Derive the governing equations of a moving fluid from three principles — mass is conserved, Newton's second law, energy is conserved — each first written for a *material volume* (a fixed set of fluid particles).
2. Turn each principle into an *integral* law for any control volume (fixed, moving steadily, accelerating, deforming) and use those laws on practical problems (wake drag, Bernoulli from a stream tube, a small bore, a rocket).
3. Build the constitutive law of a Newtonian fluid (stress linear in the strain rate, isotropic) and substitute it to get the Navier–Stokes momentum equation.
4. Show what changes when the observer's frame accelerates and rotates (Coriolis, centrifugal, frame-acceleration terms).
5. Derive the differential energy equation, separate its mechanical and thermal parts, and show how viscosity turns mechanical energy into heat (dissipation) and produces entropy.
6. Present the common special and simplified forms: angular-momentum principle, four Bernoulli equations, gravity absorbed into the pressure, the Boussinesq approximation; the boundary conditions (interfaces, no-slip, kinematic condition, surface tension).
7. Put the equations in dimensionless form and read off the numbers that control a flow (St, Re, Fr, internal Fr and Ri, Ma, Ec, Pr, We, Bo, Ca) and the meaning of dynamic similarity.

## 9. Risks, conventions, ambiguities, suspected typos

**Suspected typos / printing slips (code what is correct; record "book prints X; implemented Y"):**
1. **(4.15)** ends "… − ∫_{A*}ρu b·n dA = 0." The trailing "= 0" is wrong: (4.15) is an identity between ∫∂(ρu)/∂t and the other two terms (it is (3.35) rearranged; compare (4.3), which has no middle "=" and legitimately equals 0). With "= 0" the equation would claim ∂(ρu)/∂t integrates to zero.
2. **(4.51)** prints the two volume integrals with **dA** (∫_V ∇·q dA, ∫_V ∂q_i/∂x_i dA). Must be dV (Gauss). Harmless in (4.52), which uses dV.
3. **(4.74)** redefinition "φ → φ + ∫_{t_o}^t B(t′)dt′" has the wrong sign: to absorb B(t) one needs φ_new = φ − ∫B dt′ (then ∂φ_new/∂t = ∂φ/∂t − B and the bracket becomes 0, a constant). With "+" the right side becomes 2B(t). The conclusion (4.75) is unaffected. A test proves the printed sign fails.
4. **(4.63) discussion**: "so that μ, κ, k > 0" — κ here must be the bulk viscosity μ_v (κ is the thermal diffusivity in (4.89); the 4th edition wrote bulk viscosity as κ, a leftover). Also the second law requires ≥ 0 (non-negative), not > 0.
5. **§4.10, curve C**: "ζ = (x²/2R₁) − (y²/2R₂)" should be **+** (the cap is z = x²/2R₁ + y²/2R₂ from η = 0); the printed t and the final integrals use the + form, so only this line is wrong.
6. **Ex. 4.7**: the separated equation "2(1 − γ²/2)γ⁻¹(4 − γ²)^{−1/2}dγ = d(x/δ)" drops a minus sign (the slope is negative); the final x(ζ) formula is nevertheless correct (checked by differentiation). "evaluated at x = 0 when η = h/δ" should read γ = h/δ (η is the interface function of (4.90)). Also here **γ = ζ/δ** — yet another γ.
7. **Ex. 4.2 statement**: "(½)ρU² + gz + p/ρ = constant" is dimensionally inconsistent (J/m³ + J/kg); the derived result (4.19) is ½U² + gz + p/ρ.
8. **Cauchy prose** (after (4.24), and again in §4.8 "Recall from (4.24) that ∂τ_ij/∂x_j represents the net surface force"): the equation contracts the **first** index, ∂τ_ij/∂x_i (with f_j = n_iτ_ij). Only harmless because τ is symmetric — which the book proves *after* (4.24), in (4.25). Our code keeps the first index and a non-symmetric test τ discriminates.
9. **§4.5**: "S_mm = ∇·u is the volumetric strain rate (see Section 3.6)" — it is §3.4, (3.14) (§3.6 is the RTT).
10. **§4.8**: "multiplying (4.22) by u_j" — (4.22) is the flux form; the manipulation is direct from (4.24) (from (4.22) one must also subtract u_j × continuity). Not wrong, just longer; D32 uses (4.24) and says so.
11. **§4.11**: "The parameter groupings shown in [,]-brackets in (4.100)" → the brackets are in (4.101). "and those defined in (4.106), (4.107) becomes" (before (4.114)) → the variables are (4.109) and the equation is (4.112).

**Ambiguities to teach, not hide:**
- **γ = μ in (4.30).** The book says symmetry of σ forces γ = μ. In fact, contracting (4.29) with a *symmetric* S gives σ_ij = λS_mmδ_ij + (μ + γ)S_ij, which is already symmetric for any γ; only the sum μ + γ is physical, and "γ = μ" is the convention that names it 2μ. (Requiring K itself to be symmetric in i, j does force γ = μ — that is the book's argument, applied to K rather than to σ.) Show both views with sympy (D17 ★★★).
- **"Deviatoric stress" σ_ij (4.27)** is the viscous stress; it is traceless (a true deviator) only under the Stokes assumption μ_v = 0 or for ∇·u = 0 (tr σ = 3μ_v∇·u). State this in the notebook (many texts reserve "deviatoric" for τ + p̄δ).
- **Mean vs thermodynamic pressure** (4.33)–(4.34); incompressible p is only a mechanical pressure defined up to a constant.
- **Coriolis sign language.** The *acceleration* term is +2Ω × u′ on the left of (4.44); the Coriolis *force per mass* is −2Ω × u′ on the right of (4.45). The text's "the Coriolis acceleration 2Ω × u tends to deflect a particle to the right" really describes −2Ω × u (the next page writes −2Ω × u = −2Ω_z u_R e_φ correctly). Same trap for "centripetal" Ω × (Ω × x′) (4.43) vs "centrifugal" −Ω × (Ω × x′) (4.45). Convention callout.
- **(4.45) validity**: derived from the *incompressible, constant-μ* (4.39b) (the book says "(4.39)"), and ∇′²u′ = ∇²u needs U, Ω uniform in space (rigid frame) — true, but unstated.
- **Silently switching assumptions**: (4.19) needs steady, inviscid, constant ρ and holds *along a streamline*; the pitot paragraph calls the flow "steady and irrotational" (irrotationality is not needed for two points on one streamline); the orifice paragraph uses irrotationality to extend B to the whole tank. (4.71) is constant on streamlines *and* vortex lines; (4.72) everywhere; (4.82) along a streamline at one instant; (4.78) is not the same as (4.71) (energy, not momentum). The notebook's decision table (#123) makes the hypotheses explicit.
- **Boussinesq energy argument** uses a perfect gas (α = 1/T, R = C_p − C_v) to justify C_p; for liquids the book only says p∇·u is negligible. The ocean (Ch. 13) uses the same (4.89) with liquid properties — say so.
- **(4.9) appears twice** with different content: §4.2 Dρ/Dt = 0 (incompressible); §4.11 the identity ∇·u = −(1/ρ)Dρ/Dt = −(1/ρc²)Dp/Dt (general, isentropic). Our single row #14 records both; the notebook must not present the second as "incompressible".
- **(4.39b) appears twice** (§4.6 with D/Dt; §4.11 with ∂/∂t + (u·∇) written out) — same equation.

**Non-dimensionalisations that change inside the chapter:**
