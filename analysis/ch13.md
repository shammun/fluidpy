## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words; what is new vs restated from earlier chapters)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the five objectives):**
1. Introduce the approximations (thin layer, Boussinesq, hydrostatic, f- and β-plane, geostrophy, quasi-geostrophy) and
   the phenomena that rotation and stratification bring to large-scale flow.
2. Describe the frictional layers next to the sea surface and to solid ground in a steadily rotating frame (Ekman layers).
3. Work out what planetary rotation does to waves in homogeneous and stratified layers (Poincaré, Kelvin, inertia–gravity
   and Rossby waves; vertical normal modes; potential vorticity).
4. Describe the instabilities of the very long waves that span a range of latitude (barotropic and baroclinic instability).
5. Introduce geostrophic (two-dimensional) turbulence and its reversed energy cascade.

**Outline and what is new.**
- §13.1 Introduction — rotation + stratification as the two defining ingredients; atmosphere–ocean similarities and
  differences; z upward; northern hemisphere unless stated. *Restated*: Coriolis force (ch04 §4.7), buoyancy (ch04 §4.9, ch07).
- §13.2 Vertical variation of density — potential-density gradient (13.1), adiabatic gradients, structure of the
  atmosphere (troposphere, stratosphere) and of the ocean (mixed layer, thermocline), N² from potential density.
  *Almost all restated from ch01 §1.10*; new are the named layers and typical magnitudes.
- §13.3 Equations of motion — rotating Boussinesq set (13.2), perturbation pressure and density (13.3)–(13.4),
  anisotropic eddy-viscosity stress (13.5) and the friction force (13.6). *Restated*: (4.45), (4.86), (12.94). *New*: ν_H ≫ ν_v.
- §13.4 Thin layer on a rotating sphere — W/U ~ H/L, tangent-plane coordinates, Coriolis components (13.7), f (13.8),
  thin-shell equations (13.9), f-plane, β-plane (13.10). *New* (f was named in ch04 and ch05).
- §13.5 Geostrophic flow — balance (13.11)–(13.12), Rossby number (13.13), flow along isobars, thermal wind

  highs; Stommel's western intensification named only. *New.*

**Mental model in one sentence:** "on a rotating, stratified, thin shell the Coriolis term is as large as the pressure
gradient and the vertical balance is hydrostatic, so slow flow follows isobars (geostrophy) and its vertical shear follows
the horizontal density gradient (thermal wind); friction breaks the balance only in Ekman layers of fixed thickness
√(2ν_v/f); everything faster than f is a gravity wave bent by rotation (Poincaré, Kelvin — trapped within c/f), everything
slower is governed by potential vorticity (Rossby waves, and their instabilities when the PV gradient changes sign or the
density surfaces slope), and the turbulence that results sends energy to large scales and enstrophy to small ones."

Climate hooks for the curator (one clause each): **thermal wind** — why the jet stream sits above the strongest surface
temperature gradient; **Ekman transport** — coastal and equatorial upwelling, and with its divergence (Ekman pumping, not
in the book) the Sverdrup balance and the gyres (the book names Stommel only); **Rossby radius** — the eddy scale that
decides whether a climate-model grid resolves eddies (≈ 1000 km in the atmosphere, tens of km in the ocean);
**equivalent depth / vertical modes** — how a stratified ocean is reduced to a few shallow-water systems; **Kelvin
waves** — coastal tides and, at the equator (outside the book's analysis), El Niño's eastward signal; **Rossby waves** —
westward phase, years to cross a mid-latitude ocean, stationary waves in the westerlies; **Rayleigh–Kuo** — easterly
waves; **Eady growth rate** — the storm-track parameter 0.31 f/N · dU/dz and the poleward eddy heat flux;
**−3 enstrophy range and inverse cascade** — the observed atmospheric spectrum, zonal jets and the Rhines scale;
**lapse rates** — the troposphere sits between the dry and the moist adiabat (both conventions shown).

### 9.2 Traps (true as printed, easy to misread) — teach each with a callout
- **T1 Ω.** The book's Ω is 2π per *solar* day; the sidereal value (`ROT.OMEGA_EARTH`) is 0.27 % larger. Code uses the
  sidereal value and offers `OMEGA_SOLAR_DAY` for V6 tests. f at the poles follows.
- **T2 Lapse rates and "neutral" troposphere (the user's standing rule).** §13.2 defines no symbol Γ: it quotes rates
  of *decrease* in words (meteorological magnitudes). The project computes with Kundu's Γ ≡ dT/dz (Γ_a = −g/C_p ≈ −9.8 K/km;
  stable when dT/dz > Γ_a) and always shows the meteorological Γ ≡ −dT/dz (Γ_d ≈ +9.8 K/km; stable when Γ < Γ_d). As
  printed: (13.1) is in **potential density** (stable when dρ_θ/dz < 0); the adiabatic *temperature* gradient is named, not
  written; Fig. 13.1 plots **in-situ** temperature; "close to the moist adiabat ⇒ close to neutral" is a statement about
  saturated air — the standard troposphere is statically **stable** to dry displacements (ch01 and ch12 compute N² > 0,
  Ri > 0 for it). **Where N² appears and with which variable:** p. 625 definition, N² ≡ −(g/ρ₀)dρ/dz with ρ the
  *potential* density and ρ₀ constant (ch01 (1.29) uses ρ in the denominator and the in-situ gradient minus the adiabatic
  one — the same thing); (13.51), (13.95e), (13.133): through w dρ̄/dz = −ρ₀N²w/g, i.e. the background (potential) density;
  (13.56), (13.63), (13.65), (13.66)–(13.71): N(z) or uniform N in the mode problem; (13.96)–(13.113): N(z) of the
  internal-wave problem; (13.139), Λ = NH/f, (13.142): uniform N of the Eady problem; Exercise 13.7: N from the
  **potential-temperature** difference (N² = (g/θ)dθ/dz, `STRAT.brunt_vaisala_sq_from_theta`). No equation of the chapter
  uses in-situ temperature; the thermal wind is stated in density, with temperature only in words (row 38 supplies the
  temperature form with α as a required keyword).
- **T3 Primes dropped.** From (13.9) on, p and ρ are *perturbations* (the text above (13.9) still writes ρ′, p′);
  (13.14), (13.50), (13.95d) are hydrostatic balances of the perturbation. ρ₀ becomes ρ in (13.16)–(13.18), (13.32)–(13.34), (13.42).
- **T4 Which z.** z = 0 at the sea surface with the ocean below (§13.6, §13.9); at the solid surface (§13.7); at the
  flat bottom (§13.8); lower lid of the Eady problem (§13.17). Record per function.
- **T5 Sign of f.** Ekman formulas with √f, "ω > f", "to the right", "clockwise", e^{−fy/c} all assume f > 0. Code takes
  abs(f) and sign(f) and is tested in both hemispheres; the printed forms are kept as `*_printed` (f > 0 only).
- **T6 "Thickness" of the Ekman layer.** δ = √(2ν_v/f) is an e-folding scale; the depth at which the current first
  opposes the surface current is πδ (the oceanographic "Ekman depth"). A factor π ≈ 3 between two things with one name.
  The laminar analogue is ch08's δ_e = √(2ν/ω), against which the book's own ch08 "4√(ν/ω)" was already a recorded trap.
- **T7 Orbit vs hodograph.** Fig. 13.15's axes are velocity amplitudes; the particle path has the same axis ratio and
  sense but its axes are those lengths divided by ω. §13.11 quotes the axis ratio as ω/f (major/minor), §13.14 as f/ω
  (minor/major) for the same ellipse.
- **T8 Rossby-wave signs.** (13.118) with "ω regarded as positive" forces k < 0; "eastward phase speed" c_x is negative;
  its "maximum" is a maximum of magnitude. Group velocity: westward for long waves (K² < f₀²/c²), eastward for short
  ones; arrows in the lower panel of Fig. 13.28 point toward the centre of the circles. Code returns signed ω for signed k.
- **T9 "All roots real; two superinertial (ω ≫ f), one subinertial (ω ≪ f)".** True under β-plane scaling; the strong

### 9.4 Numerical risks
- Eady closed form near αH → 0 (0/0) and near the cut-off (square root of a number crossing zero): evaluate the radicand
  as a product, series below x = 1e-3, never join c_i across the cut-off with a line.
- Vertical modes: the barotropic and first baroclinic speeds differ by two orders of magnitude; with a free surface use
  the scaled eigenvalue 1/c² and check the Robin row; spurious high modes — keep only those unchanged under grid doubling.
- Shallow-water model: CFL set by √(gH) (hundreds of m/s for an external mode — use an equivalent depth for long runs);
  a C-grid needs Coriolis averaging (state which conservation property the chosen average has); PV on particles needs
  interpolation of matching order.
- Cubic dispersion relation: the Rossby root is ~10⁻³ of the others — use the trigonometric solution, not a general
  polynomial solver, and test with relative tolerances per root.
- `rayleigh_kuo_eigs`: all ch11 rules (leading mode or raise; box max(profile box, 12/k); complex path near neutrality).
- 2-D turbulence: de-alias; seed; the −3 slope needs resolution the notebook budget does not allow — show it with its
  fit window and the label "qualitative".
- Functions with f in a denominator raise at the equator rather than return inf.
