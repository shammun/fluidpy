## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the chapter objectives):**
1. Give the vocabulary of vortical flow — vortex lines, vortex tubes and their strength, vortex sheets — and show how these objects behave with and without viscosity.
2. Derive and state the classical results on how vorticity is produced, carried, turned, stretched and diffused: Kelvin's circulation theorem, Helmholtz's vortex theorems, and the vorticity equation, first in an inertial frame with constant density, then in a steadily rotating frame with variable density (Boussinesq).
3. Derive the Biot–Savart law: vorticity at one place sets the velocity everywhere else.
4. Use it to explain striking vortex behaviour: pairs that orbit or travel together, a vortex that slides along a wall (images), vortex rings approaching a wall and leap-frogging, and the tangential-velocity jump across a vortex sheet.

**Outline:** 5.1 vorticity as twice the spin; solid-body rotation (5.1) and the ideal (irrotational) line vortex (5.2); vortex lines (5.3); vortex tubes (Fig. 5.1), their strength and Gauss on a tube (5.4) (tubes cannot end in the fluid); viscosity in the two basic vortices: solid body in a tank (5.5a, b) → paraboloid isobars (5.6) (Fig. 5.2), Bernoulli function varies across streamlines; line vortex: nonzero σ_rθ but zero net viscous force, isobars (5.7) (Fig. 5.3), Bernoulli everywhere; rotating cylinder u_θ = ωa²/2r = Rankine vortex, torque carried to infinity; "irrotational ≠ stress-free, = no net viscous force" · 5.2 Kelvin's theorem (5.8), proof (5.9)–(5.11) with Fig. 5.4; the three sources of vorticity (nonconservative body forces — Coriolis drain vortex; ## 9. Risks, conventions, ambiguities, suspected typos

**Suspected typos / printing slips (code what is correct; record "book prints X; implemented Y"):**
1. **(5.14) sign.** Book prints u = **−**(1/4π)∫(∇′ × ω)/\lvert x − x′\rvert d³x′. From ∇²u = −∇ × ω and the Green's function of its own Exercise 5.9 (φ = −(1/4π)∫q/\lvert x − x′\rvert d³x′ solves ∇²φ = q) with q = −∇′ × ω, the factor is **+1/(4π)**. The next displayed identity has a compensating slip: it prints "+ ((x − x′)/\lvert x − x′\rvert³) × ω(x′, t)" where the product rule with ∇′(1/\lvert x − x′\rvert) = +(x − x′)/\lvert x − x′\rvert³ (checked with sympy today) gives "−((x − x′)/\lvert x − x′\rvert³) × ω" = "+ω × (x − x′)/\lvert x − x′\rvert³". The two slips cancel, so the book's following line (second integral) and (5.16)–(5.17) are right (checked today: a straight filament gives Γ/2πr), but the first integral of that line also carries the wrong sign (harmless — it vanishes for the chosen V′). Test: the printed (5.14) reverses the swirl of a Gaussian tube.
2. **"(see Exercise 5.8)"** after (5.14) should be **Exercise 5.9** (the Green's function exercise; 5.8 is Crocco–Vazsonyi).
3. **(5.33) "(see Exercise 5.11)"** should be **Exercise 5.10** (5.11 is Hill's vortex).
4. **§5.4 Lamb step**: "∇ × {∇(u·u) + ω × u}" should read ∇(½u·u); harmless (curl of a gradient).
5. **(5.26) discussion**: "(u_j²/2 + Π)_{,iq}" — Π is a slip for Φ.
6. **(5.27)** drops the term u_{j,j}(ω_n + 2Ω_n) between the second and third lines without comment (zero by (5.19)); the "where we have used u_{i,i} = 0" note after it covers it only implicitly.
7. **Fig. 5.16 caption** prints dΓ/ds = u₁ − u₂ while the text gives Γ ≡ dΓ/ds = u₂ − u₁ (u₁ above, u₂ below). The text's value is the counterclockwise circulation per length (negative for the clockwise filaments drawn); the caption's is its clockwise magnitude. Code: counterclockwise-positive γ = u_below − u_above with a `convention=` switch; teach both.
8. **Fig. 5.2** labels the tank's rotation "2ω", but with (5.1) u_θ = ωr/2 the tank turns at **ω/2** (ω is the vorticity); the label looks inherited from an edition where ω was the rotation rate. Exercise 5.1's hint (h = ω₀²r²/2g with ω₀ the tank rate) is consistent with (5.6) because ω = 2ω₀.
9. **Kelvin proof justification**: "the first term … is zero because C is closed, and ρ and p are single valued" — single-valuedness is not enough; the integral vanishes because for barotropic ρ = ρ(p) the integrand dp/ρ is the exact differential of the pressure function (4.67). A baroclinic counterexample (single-valued p and ρ, nonzero ∮dp/ρ) is in the tests (#12).
10. **"C lies entirely in irrotational fluid ⇒ no net viscous force"**: true for incompressible constant-μ flow ((4.40): μ∇²u = −μ∇ × ω). For compressible flow the viscous force has an extra (μ_v + 4μ/3)∇(∇·u) part that does not vanish with ω; the book's (5.11) is general but the remark is incompressible-only.
11. **"Hyperboloids of revolution of the second degree"** (Fig. 5.3): the isobars (c − z)r² = const are cubic surfaces, not quadric hyperboloids; teach the shape from the formula, not the name.
12. **§5.2 references (4.39) and (4.59)**: (4.39b) is the constant-μ incompressible form, while (5.10) uses the general viscous stress σ_ij of (4.59) — effectively Cauchy (4.24) with τ = −pδ + σ. Also (5.10) differentiates σ on the second index (∂σ_ij/∂x_j) where ch04's convention is the first; harmless because σ is symmetric (CUMULATIVE "first index" note).

**Conventions and silent switches:**
- **ω means vorticity** in (5.1) (u_θ = ωr/2), **not** the rotation rate; ch03's `solid_body_rotation(r, omega0)` takes the rotation rate (ω = 2ω₀). A convention callout with numbers: tank at 1 rad/s ⇒ ω = 2 s⁻¹, (5.6) coefficient ρω²/8 = ρ(1)²/2. `ch05.solid_body_from_vorticity` converts; the WV test catches passing ω as ω₀.
- **Γ has five meanings by now**: lapse rate (ch01), a shear-rate preset (ch02/ch03), circulation (ch03 →), **vortex-sheet strength per length (§5.8, printed Γ; exercises γ)**. Code: `Gamma` = circulation only; `gamma` = sheet strength [m/s]. γ also = ratio of specific heats, shear rate, a (4.29) coefficient, ζ/δ.
- **σ**: viscous stress σ_ij (ch04/§5.1–5.2) vs vortex core radius (ch03) vs surface tension (ch01); **§5.6 comma notation**; ε used both as Levi-Civita ε_ijk and dissipation ε (D08).
- **Two derivations of the same equation with different hypotheses**: §5.4 (constant ρ, inertial, vector route) vs §5.6 (Boussinesq with variable ρ in the pressure term, rotating, index route). (5.30) with Ω = 0 and ∇ρ × ∇p = 0 must reduce to (5.13) — asserted. The "Boussinesq" label with the full 1/ρ in the pressure term and a constant ν is a mixed approximation (D20): the baroclinic term is ∇ρ × ∇p/ρ² ≈ ∇ρ′ × g/ρ₀ to leading order (cf. (4.86)); say so when teaching and in the docstring.
- **u and ω relative vs absolute** in (5.30): in the rotating frame they are relative; the stretching term acts on the **absolute** vorticity ω + 2Ω; with Ω = 0 they are absolute. Planetary stretching needs only *fluid* lines to stretch.
- **Orientation**: 2-D vorticity and circulation counterclockwise positive about +z; the vortex-tube strength sign depends on the loop orientation (Stokes: n_c into A, ch02). The lower-end term of (5.4) is −Γ_lower because the outward normal there points against ω.
- **Point vortices**: no self-induced velocity for a straight line vortex; a curved filament (ring) has a logarithmically singular self-induction — needs a core size a (Kelvin's formula); the book's rings are qualitative. Choose `core="uniform"` (constant ¼) and state it.
- **Image systems** satisfy only the inviscid (no-penetration) condition, not no-slip; the wall "vortex drift Γ/4πh" is inviscid physics.
- **Nothing in this chapter is non-dimensionalised**; all functions are dimensional SI; explainers may use Γ = 2π, h = 1 units.

**Numerical risks:**
- Nested stencils (ω from u, then ∇²ω) amplify round-off: h ≈ 1e-3·L with 2nd-order stencils gives ~1e-6 relative residuals; tests measure the order, never compare at a single h; sympy for exact identities.
- Biot–Savart kernels are singular at x = x′: smoothed kernel ε for points inside the vorticity, filament evaluation excluded on the filament itself; the ring self-velocity uses the Kelvin formula, not the kernel.
- Elliptic integrals: `scipy.special.ellipk(m)` takes the **parameter m = k²**, not the modulus k (a classic factor bug) — test against the polygon Biot–Savart.
- Point-vortex close encounters (leap-frogging rings, knife pair near the wall): use DOP853 with rtol 1e-11 and assert invariants; stop with an event if two vortices come closer than a core size.
- Material loops stretch exponentially in strain flows: insert points when the spacing exceeds a threshold (or keep the run short) so the periodic quadrature stays accurate; report the loop length.
- Series (channel images, Exercise 5.13): sum until the increment < tol, never a fixed count (ch04 lesson); the sum converges like 1/N.
