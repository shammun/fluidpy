## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the five chapter objectives):**
1. Set up the field equation and the boundary conditions — at a free surface, at an interface between two liquids and in the interior of a stratified fluid — that govern gravity waves, and show how small amplitude makes them linear.
2. For a liquid layer of any constant depth, derive the linear gravity–capillary wave: how fast it travels, what pressure it produces under the surface, why different wavelengths separate (dispersion), how the fluid particles move, and how much energy the wave holds and carries.
3. Explain refraction by depth changes, and what happens when amplitude is no longer small, in shallow water (steepening, breaking, hydraulic jumps, solitary waves) and in deep water (Stokes waves, Stokes drift).
4. Find the linear waves on a density interface, first between two deep fluids and then with a free surface above a layer (the barotropic and baroclinic modes).
5. Describe internal gravity waves in a fluid with constant buoyancy frequency N: frequency set by direction, not wavelength; motion along the crests; group velocity at right angles to the phase velocity.

**Outline (with equation ranges):** 7.1 wave vocabulary — amplitude, wavelength, wavenumber, period, frequency, phase speed, the wavenumber vector, trace velocities, Doppler shift (7.1)–(7.9) · 7.2 linear free-surface problem (7.10)–(7.21), separable solution and the dispersion relation (7.22)–(7.29), pressure (7.30)–(7.31), particle orbits and streamlines (7.32)–(7.37), energy and energy flux (7.38)–(7.44), deep and shallow limits (7.45)–(7.52), refraction (Figs. 7.8–7.9) · 7.3 surface tension: the Laplace-jump boundary condition (7.53)–(7.55), capillary–gravity dispersion and the minimum phase speed (7.56)–(7.60) · 7.4 standing waves and seiches (7.61)–(7.65) · 7.5 group velocity from beats and packets (7.66)–(7.71), the stone-in-a-pond wave train, kinematic wave theory: crest conservation and rays (7.72)–(7.79) · 7.6 nonlinear shallow-water steepening, hydraulic jumps (7.80)–(7.81) and their energy loss, Stokes waves (7.82)–(7.83), Stokes drift (7.84)–(7.86), Korteweg–de Vries and the solitary wave (7.87)–(7.88) · 7.7 interface between two deep fluids (7.89)–(7.96), layer with a free surface over a deep fluid (7.97)–(7.119) · 7.8 linear Boussinesq equations for a stratified fluid (7.120)–(7.134), internal-wave dispersion (7.135)–(7.142), phase and group velocity (7.143)–(7.146), energy (7.147)–(7.159) · 20 exercises.

## 9. Risks, conventions, ambiguities, suspected typos

**Conventions (record in `knowledge/notation.md`)**
- **R1 η changes meaning.** ch04 (4.90) η is a surface *function* (surface = {η = 0}, n = ∇η/\|∇η\|); here η(x, t) is the surface *elevation* and the surface function is f = z − η (7.14). `core.IF.kinematic_bc_residual` must be called with η_imp = z − η (as `ch04.linear_wave_fields` already does). Never pass the elevation as the level-set function.
- **R2 coordinates.** z up, still surface z = 0, bottom z = −H (Fig. 7.2); §7.7 two-layer problem: origin at the mean free surface, interface at z = −H (7.97)–(7.101); the text of §7.1 says "undisturbed … location y = 0" (T5). Horizontal ψ convention u = ∂ψ/∂z, w = −∂ψ/∂x (ch04's with y → z; GFD's opposite sign is a Ch. 13 trap).
- **R3 symbol clashes inside the chapter.** ω = angular frequency (vorticity in ch02–ch06); ζ = vertical particle excursion (§7.2, §7.6, (7.149)) *and* interface displacement (§7.7) (relative vorticity in ch04/ch13); θ = local phase θ(x, t) (7.72) *and* angle of K above the horizontal (7.139); ε² = density ratio (7.95) (dissipation ε in ch04); b = interface amplitude (7.103) (buoyancy b in ch04); A, B, C reused for different constants in §7.2, §7.7; m = vertical wavenumber (mass elsewhere); k = wavenumber (conductivity ch01, ch06 half-body ratio); σ = surface tension (vortex core ch03). Code names: `omega`, `zeta_particle` vs `zeta_interface`, `theta_phase` vs `theta_K`, `eps2_density`.
- **R4 complex notation from §7.7 on**: Re{} dropped (7.89); products (energy, flux) need real parts first — ⟨Re A e^{iθ} · Re B e^{iθ}⟩ = ½Re(AB*) — the book states this only at (7.154). ρ′ is 90° out of phase with w (factor i in (7.153)).
- **R5 energy normalisations switch**: surface/interfacial E, E_k, E_p are per unit **horizontal area** (depth-integrated) and F per unit crest **length**; internal-wave E is per unit **volume** and F per unit **area** (the book says so after (7.159)). Surface E uses $\overline{\eta^2}$ (= a²/2 only for a sinusoid).
- **R6 g**: the book uses 9.81; `core` defaults to G0 = 9.80665. Chapter functions default to `G_BOOK`; tests pass g explicitly (CUMULATIVE pitfall).
- **R7 reduced gravity**: (7.117) g′ = g(ρ₂ − ρ₁)/ρ₂; `core.SIM.reduced_gravity` (ch04) uses ρ₁. Differ by ρ₂/ρ₁ (0.3 % in the ocean, 20 % for oil–water-like demos). Add `ref="lower"|"upper"` (backwards compatible) or a separate `reduced_gravity_book`; state the choice wherever g′ appears (Ch. 13 uses ρ₀).
- **R8 intrinsic vs observed frequency** (7.9): all later ω are intrinsic. The book's "c₀ = c + U" is loose (a phase velocity does not add like a vector — trace-velocity issue); only ω₀ = ω + U·K is safe to code.
- **R9 sign of k in internal waves**: (7.138) ω = kN/K and (7.145) c_g = (Nm/K³)(m e_x − k e_z) assume **k > 0**; Fig. 7.29 draws K pointing up-left (k < 0), where the printed (7.145) would point c_g up-right instead of the drawn down-left. Code ω = \|k\|N/K and c_g = ∇_K ω (sign-safe); test the Fig. 7.29 configuration. θ from cos θ = \|k\|/K, not tan⁻¹(m/k).
- **R10 beam angle wording**: θ is K's angle to the horizontal = the beam (c_g, particle motion) angle to the **vertical**; the Fig. 7.33 caption says "45° with the horizontal" — identical only because 45° is symmetric. Explainers must label both angles.
- **R11 regimes**: book thresholds deep kH > 2 (H > 0.32λ, "one third") at ~2 %, shallow H < 0.07λ at ~3 %. `depth_regime` returns the error, never just the label.

**Assumptions that switch silently**
- §7.2 linear, σ = 0, air ignored → §7.3 adds σ (dispersion only) → §7.5 slowly varying trains (WKB-like, not stated as such) and inhomogeneous H(x) → §7.6 finite amplitude (qualitative for shallow water; Stokes series for deep; KdV only for λ/H ≈ 10–20) → §7.7 two immiscible fluids, lower layer infinitely deep, σ = 0, complex notation → §7.8 Boussinesq, continuous N, rotational flow (no φ), constant N for the plane waves (N(z) allowed in (7.134)).
- Energy integrals are taken to z = 0, not η (consistent at second order; stated for (7.38) only).
- The hydraulic-jump energy E = u²/2 + gH is per unit mass *of a surface particle*; head loss = ΔE/g.

**Numerical risks**
- cosh/sinh overflow for kH ≳ 350: use the e^{kz}(1 + e^{−2k(z+H)})/(1 − e^{−2kH}) form; `np.tanh` is safe.
- Inverse dispersion: bracket from physics (deep root ω²/g and shallow root ω/√(gH) bound the true k); assert the residual.
- Complex-step c_g needs ω written with complex-safe numpy (no `abs`, no `np.sqrt` of negative reals — use `np.emath` or analytic branches); fall back to 4th-order central differences.
- KdV: stiff third derivative → integrating factor; dealias 2/3; time step limited by the nonlinear term; check invariants every run.
- FFT evolution is periodic: the domain must be long enough that the fastest component (c_g,max t) does not wrap; the ω(k = 0) mode must be handled (η̂_t0/ω → η̂_t0 · t limit).
- Stokes series (7.82) is only asymptotic: restrict ka ≤ 0.3 in explainers; do not claim the 120° crest from the truncated series.
- Stokes-drift numerics need long integrations with tight tolerances (drift is O((ka)²) of the orbit): DOP853, rtol 1e-10, ≥ 10 periods, subtract the linear orbit.
- ρ₁ > ρ₂ in `interface_omega` gives imaginary ω (Rayleigh–Taylor instability, Ch. 11) — return NaN with a warning, never a silent real number.
