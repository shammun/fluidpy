## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words; what is new vs restated from earlier chapters)
The chapter (written by P. S. Ayyaswamy) is a survey: it introduces the vocabulary of physiology, then re-uses the
whole book — Poiseuille flow, Stokes layers, d'Alembert waves, influence coefficients, curved-pipe secondary flow,
lubrication-style expansions, creeping flow — on blood and sap. The objectives, in our words:
1. Learn the language: heart chambers, valves, systole/diastole, stroke volume, cardiac output, hematocrit, compliance,
   peripheral resistance, the pressure–volume loop.
2. Know the numbers of the human circulation (pressures, speeds, vessel sizes, Reynolds and Womersley numbers).
3. Model steady and pulsatile blood flow analytically: Poiseuille and its resistance; two-layer (cell-free layer)
   flow; a distensible tube; the Windkessel; pulse waves (Moens–Korteweg); Womersley's oscillatory pipe flow; viscous
   waves in an elastic tube; Casson (yield-stress) flow.
4. See how tube properties change the flow: rigid vs elastic vs collapsible walls, branching (Murray's law, wave
   reflection at junctions), curvature (Dean flow).
5. Overview transport in plants: water potential, xylem (transpiration, tension) and phloem (pressure-flow, sieve
   plates).

Outline: §16.1 Introduction (prose) · §16.2 The circulatory system — circulations, the heart as a pump, work of the
ventricle (PV loop), SV/EF/CO/CI (16.1)–(16.4), nature of blood and its rheology (16.5)–(16.7), Fåhræus–Lindqvist
bookkeeping (16.8), vessels, total peripheral resistance (16.9)–(16.11) · §16.3 Modeling of flow in blood vessels —
Womersley number (16.12); steady Poiseuille recap (16.13)–(16.20); two-layer FL model (16.21)–(16.36); entrance length
(16.37); elastic-tube Poiseuille (16.38)–(16.46); Windkessel (16.47)–(16.52); inviscid pulse waves (16.53)–(16.68);
Womersley rigid-tube flow (16.69)–(16.100); viscous waves in an elastic tube (16.101)–(16.136); viscoelastic wall
(16.137); Murray's law (16.138)–(16.147); reflection at junctions (16.148)–(16.163); curved tube/Dean (16.164)–(16.182);
collapsible tubes, Korotkoff, Starling resistor, Shapiro's 1-D theory (16.183)–(16.206); Casson pipe flow
(16.207)–(16.220); pulmonary circulation (prose) · §16.4 Plants — water potential (16.221), xylem, phloem, sieve-plate
formula (16.222) · Exercises 16.1–16.6.

**Restated** (SEEN): Hagen–Poiseuille (ch08 (8.6)–(8.8)); Stokes' second problem (ch08 (8.33)–(8.38)) as the large-α
limit; continuity and Navier–Stokes in cylindrical coordinates (ch04); d'Alembert solution and p′ = ρc u′ (ch15
(15.15)–(15.16)); one-dimensional duct equations and influence coefficients with a critical speed (ch15 (15.18)–(15.26),
(15.32)–(15.34), (15.46)–(15.48)); Reynolds and Strouhal numbers (ch04); hydraulic diameter and friction coefficient
(ch15 §15.6); entrance length (ch08 sketch, ch09); Taylor–Couette/Dean flows named (ch11 N69); stream function (ch04);
regular perturbation series (ch08 lubrication, ch09); hydrostatics (ch01). **New**: physiology vocabulary; non-Newtonian
constitutive laws (Casson, yield stress, apparent viscosity); hematocrit bookkeeping and the two-layer model; tube laws
(hoop tension, bending/collapse); compliance and the Windkessel ODE; Moens–Korteweg; Womersley's Bessel-function solution;
membrane equations of an elastic wall and the dispersion relation (16.136); minimum-work optimisation (Murray); impedance
and junction reflection; toroidal coordinates and Dean's expansion; the speed index S; Casson plug flow; water
potential and phloem sieve plates. **Bessel functions of complex argument are the main new maths tool.**

### Traps and conventions
- **T1 Two time conventions.** Rigid-tube Womersley uses e^{+iωt} (16.73)–(16.75) ⇒ argument i^{3/2}α; the elastic-tube
  analysis uses e^{i(kx − ωt)} (16.116) ⇒ β = √(iω/ν) = i^{1/2}α/a. Complex conjugates of each other; never mix in code.
  E* = E − iωE′ (16.137) is Kelvin–Voigt only in the e^{−iωt} convention.
- **T2 Overloaded symbols** (code names in brackets): α = Womersley number [`Wo`], Shapiro area ratio A/A₀
  [`area_ratio`], toroidal angle [`alpha_t`], α_c Charm–Kurland, compliance in Ex. 16.1; K = compliance (16.48)
  [`compliance`], metabolic cost per volume (16.138) [`b_metabolic`], Dean's K (16.178) [`K_dean`], K_p bending stiffness,
  K_c Casson (dimensional), k_c Casson (dimensionless); R = peripheral resistance [`R_periph`], radius of curvature
  [`R_curv`], reflection coefficient [`refl`], viscous resistance per unit length in (16.204) [`R_per_length`], gas
  constant [`R_U`]; δ = cell-free layer (16.21ff) and curvature ratio (16.164); ψ = secondary stream function and water
  potential; c = wave speed and concentration (16.221); T = period, temperature, tensions T_θ, T_x; M = Shapiro's
  coefficient (not Mach); S = speed index, s = perimeter and arc length; P = Shapiro's dimensionless pressure, P̂(A) tube
  law, P_I, P_R amplitudes; X, Y = Re, Im of 1 − F₂ and X also our k²Eh/(2ρa₀ω²) [`Xk`]; κ = Dean number (ch12: von
  Kármán); λ = wavelength and curved/straight resistance ratio; E = Young's modulus and Ê energy rate; H = hematocrit
  (ch07/ch13: depth); h = wall thickness, η wall radial displacement (ch07: elevation); ν kinematic viscosity vs ν̂
  Poisson ratio [`poisson`].
- **T3 Units.** Book mixes SI, CGS (poise, dyn/cm²), mmHg, cm³ bar/(mol K), PRU (mmHg s/cm³). Code SI; converters
  `mmHg_to_Pa` (133.322387415), `cP_to_Pa_s` (1e-3), `PRU` (1.33322e8 Pa s/m³), `bar_to_Pa`. Slip S27 is a units slip.
- **T4 Pressure gradient sign.** G = −dp/dx (amplitude) as ch08 `G=`; Δp = p₁ − p₂ > 0; Shapiro's p₀ = −Gs.
- **T5 Stress sign.** §16.3 uses τ = −μ du/dr > 0; ch08 code uses signed τ = μ du/dR. Functions return the positive
  magnitude and say so.
- **T6 Dimensionless groups change definitions.** Re = 2au/ν (diameter), St = 2aω/u ⇒ α² = Re·St/4; Wikipedia α² = 2π
  Re St with other Re, St; Exercise 16.2 St = aω/U; α uses the radius (some literature uses the diameter).
- **T7 Root choice in (16.136).** Two roots; the pressure wave is the root with X → 1 as α → ∞ (c → c₀ of (16.62)). The
  other has c ≫ c₀ and large damping (a wall-dominated mode with wall inertia neglected) — compute both, label, select by
  continuity from large α; at small α both are heavily damped. Analyst's numbers (ν̂ = 0.5): α = 8 → c/c₀ = 0.954,
  k₂/k₁ = 0.062; α = 20 → 0.981, 0.022.
- **T8 Dean numbers.** κ (16.171), K = 2κ² (16.178), D = 4√2 κ (16.179), K = D²/16; the literature's De = Re√δ (with
  diameter Re) equals κ here. The validity range of the small-D series is quoted by the book (private).
- **T9 Shapiro's P is negative in collapse.** −P ≈ α^{−n} − 1 > 0 for α < 1. Every statement about the sign of P dK_p/dx
  flips with P (S24). For the power law, P′ = nα^{−n−1}, P″ = −n(n + 1)α^{−n−2}, so M = 3 − (n + 1) = 2 − n = ½ for
  n = 3/2 (constant) — check this in the test, it simplifies (16.205).
- **T10 S plays M's role.** At S = 1 RHS(16.205) = −M·RHS(16.204); choking/flow limitation. Form the driver before
  dividing by 1 − S² (ch15 lesson).
- **T11 PV loop orientation.** The cycle is counterclockwise in (V horizontal, p vertical), so ∮p dV < 0; work on the
  blood is −∮p dV > 0.
- **T12 Windkessel pressures** are transmural with p̄_V = 0 and zero outside pressure; p₀ = end-diastolic pressure.
- **T13 Hematocrit** fraction (code) vs percent (prose); (16.5) requires H > 0.1 as a fraction.
- **T14 Casson constants.** K_c = k_c√μ_p has units (Pa s)^{1/2}; Whitmore's 1.53 is k_c (dimensionless) and 2.0 is
  √(τ_y/μ_p) in s^{-1/2}.
- **T15 MAP ⅓ rule** is an estimate for a typical waveform, not a definition of the mean.
- **T16 Tube-law singularity** (16.41): a → ∞ as a₀(p − p_e)/Eh → 1 (a consequence of using the deformed radius in
  the hoop stress with a linear law) — raise, do not return negative radii.
- **T17 Moens–Korteweg radius vs diameter.** Eh/(2ρa) = Eh/(ρd); (16.63) mid-wall variant.
- **T18 Junction "closed end"** means R = +1 (pressure doubles, flow reverses); "open end" R = −1.
- **T19 FL closure is ours.** The book leaves δ and μ_c undetermined; any μ_app(D) curve depends on our choice of δ
  (label "model, ours"); Pries' law is the measured comparison.
- **T20 Fig. 16.14 axis** runs from wall (1) through the centre (0) and is mirrored; draw the full diameter and say so.
- **T21 Casson plug.** a_c ≥ a ⇒ no flow; Δp_min = 2Lτ_y/a.
- **T22 Water potential sign.** ψ < 0 in xylem under tension; the osmotic term −RTc lowers ψ; ψ = 0 for pure water at
  atmospheric pressure at z = 0.
- **T23 Rigid-tube Womersley is not a wave**: infinite wave speed, u independent of x; waves need the elastic wall
  (16.101)ff.
- **T24 Equal-volume sphere comparison and other "ours" demos** must be labelled ours, with cited (not book) inputs.
- **T25 Large-α Bessel overflow**: J₀(i^{3/2}α) grows like e^{α/√2}; use `scipy.special.jve` (scaled) ratios or the
  asymptote beyond α ≈ 50.
- **T27 Apparent viscosity: secant or tangent?** The book calls μ_app "the slope at a point" but defines it through
  τ/γ̇ (the secant, as in (16.32)); for a Casson fluid the tangent dτ/dγ̇ is smaller. Code `apparent_viscosity` = τ/γ̇
  and `differential_viscosity` = dτ/dγ̇ (add to I4); plot both once.
- **T26 Equations stated without derivation**: (16.11), (16.36), (16.37), (16.63), (16.137), (16.146) (only "making
  displacements"), (16.178), (16.188), (16.204)–(16.206), (16.222). The curator should decide which to write out (we
  re-derived (16.146), (16.178)'s coefficient and (16.204)–(16.206)).
