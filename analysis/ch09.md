## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the six objectives):**
1. State the boundary-layer idea (viscosity matters only in a thin layer at high Re) and show which terms of the Navier–Stokes equations survive inside it.
2. Give the resulting equations of an *attached laminar* boundary layer and the conditions that close them (no slip, matching to the outer potential flow, an inlet profile).
3. Provide exact and approximate solutions: Blasius (flat plate), Falkner–Skan (power-law outer flow), the von Kármán integral equation and Thwaites' one-parameter method.
4. Describe transition to turbulence and separation — what they do to drag and why an adverse pressure gradient causes separation.
5. Describe the Reynolds-number-dependent flow past bluff bodies (cylinder, sphere, sports balls): wake regimes, vortex streets, the drag crisis, why balls swing.
6. Show the boundary-layer method also works without a wall (free jet, wall jet) and hint at secondary flows (the teacup).

**Outline with equation ranges:** 9.1 Prandtl's hypothesis; scaling (9.1)–(9.6); scaled equations (9.7)–(9.8); the equations of the layer (9.9)–(9.10); matching (9.11); boundary conditions (9.12)–(9.15); the parabolic character of the layer; skin-friction estimate · 9.2 three thicknesses: δ₉₉, displacement δ* (9.16), momentum θ (9.17) · 9.3 Blasius: (9.18)–(9.29) similarity reduction, thicknesses, τ₀, C_f, C_D (9.30)–(9.33) · 9.4 Falkner–Skan (9.34)–(9.36); Fig. 9.7; separation at n = −0.0904 · 9.5 von Kármán momentum integral (9.37)–(9.43) · 9.6 Thwaites (9.44)–(9.50), Table 9.1, Examples 9.1 (Blasius) and 9.2 (diffuser) · 9.7 transition, pressure-gradient effect on profile curvature (9.51)–(9.52), separation, form drag · 9.8 cylinder from Re < 1 to 10⁶ (Figs. 9.16–9.21, vortex street, Strouhal number, drag crisis) · 9.9 sphere and sports balls (cricket swing, tennis and baseball spin) · 9.10 laminar free jet (9.53)–(9.76) and wall jet (9.77)–(9.85) · 9.11 secondary flows (teacup) · 28 exercises.

## 9. Risks, conventions, ambiguities, suspected typos

**Printed slips (checked; code the corrected form, keep the printed form as a named wrong variant a test must fail):**
- **R1 — (9.7)** prints ∂²u\*/∂x\* and ∂²u\*/∂y\* with denominators that lack the squares (image at 220 dpi: "∂x\*", "∂y\*"), while (9.8) prints ∂x\*², ∂y\*². Dimensionally and by scaling the correct terms are (1/Re)∂²u\*/∂x\*² + ∂²u\*/∂y\*². The coefficient placement (1/Re only on the x-derivative) is correct.
- **R2 — (9.30)** η₉₉ = 4.93 is read off the figure; the exact root of f′ = 0.99 is **4.910** (0.4 % smaller). δ\* = 1.7208, θ = 0.6641 (book 1.72, 0.664) are fine. The text's "Re_x = 6×10⁴, δ₉₉ = 2 cm for air at 1 m/s and 1 m" is 2 cm only for ν ≈ 1.6×10⁻⁵ (ν = 1.5×10⁻⁵ gives 6.7×10⁴ and 1.9 cm).

**Conventions and ambiguities the design must keep straight:**
- **Reference thickness names:** δ̄ (order-of-magnitude, §9.1), δ (similarity length √(νx/U) — *not* δ₉₉), δ₉₉, δ\* (displacement; the star also marks dimensionless variables in (9.6)–(9.8)), θ (momentum thickness; an angle everywhere else in the book, incl. cylinder angle φ and sphere θ), h₉₉ (jet). Say which in every axis label; η = y/δ = y√(U/νx) (Blasius) but η = y/√(νt) in ch08 (t ↔ x/U: a factor exactly 1) and y/(2√(νt)) on the book's ch08 figures.
- **Angles on a cylinder or sphere:** the book measures separation from the **forward** stagnation point (82°, 125°, 85°, 120°); ch06 measured θ from +x (downstream); ch08 sphere from the downstream axis; Exercise 9.21 uses φ from the upstream stagnation point with x = aφ. `BB` takes `phi_deg` = angle from the forward stagnation point and says so.
- **Reynolds numbers (six):** overall Re = U∞L/ν (9.7), Re_x = Ux/ν, Re_L, Re_δ-type in jets (Re_x = xu₀/ν, Re_h99), cylinder/sphere **diameter** Re = U∞d/ν (ch08 sphere Re on the diameter; Re_a on the radius), and Falkner–Skan Re_x = ax^{n+1}/ν. Never mix.
- **Pressure gradient sign:** dp/dx > 0 adverse (ch08's `dpdx` book sign, favourable < 0); (9.35) −dp/dx = na²x^{2n−1}; wall curvature (9.51)–(9.52) follows dp/dx.
- **ψ sign:** u = ∂ψ/∂y, v = −∂ψ/∂x (ch04, ch08); jets symmetric ψ is odd in η.
- **C constants:** C is used for (i) a proportionality in δ (Blasius), (ii) ∫f′²dη = 4√6/3 (free jet), (iii) u₀ = Cx^{-1/2} (wall jet, dimensional); C₁, C₂, C₃ integration constants; C_f, C_D coefficients. `ch09` uses `C_fj`/`C_wj` in code.
