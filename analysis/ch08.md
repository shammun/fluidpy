## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the four chapter objectives):**
1. Solve the full viscous equations — Navier–Stokes with no-slip walls — exactly or nearly exactly for simple geometries, both between walls (confined) and in open fluid (unconfined).
2. Introduce lubrication theory: why a thin gap turns Navier–Stokes into a local pressure–friction balance, and what that buys (bearings that carry loads, Hele-Shaw cells that mimic ideal flow, spreading films).
3. Explain what a similarity solution is — a solution in which space and time only appear in one combination — and how to find one for exact and approximate viscous equations.
4. Derive the creeping-flow (Stokes) equations for Re → 0 and use them for the sphere: streamlines, pressure, the drag 6πμaU, and the limits of the theory (Oseen).

**Outline (with equation ranges):** 8.1 laminar vs turbulent (Reynolds's dye experiment, Re ≈ 2000–3000 in a pipe), ν as the diffusivity of vorticity and momentum, the governing equations (8.1) and the wall conditions (8.2)–(8.3) · 8.2 steady exact solutions whose advective term vanishes by symmetry: plane Couette–Poiseuille (8.4)–(8.5) with flow rate and shear stress, round-pipe Poiseuille (8.6)–(8.8) with flow rate, circular Couette flow between rotating cylinders (8.9)–(8.10) and its two limits, the viscous-but-irrotational vortex (8.11) and solid-body rotation (8.12) · 8.3 lubrication: the governing equations (8.13), the thin-gap scaling (8.14) and scaled equations (8.15)–(8.16), the zeroth-order lubrication balance (8.17), the generic gap profile (8.18)–(8.19); Example 8.1 slider bearing (pressure and load), Example 8.2 Hele-Shaw flow (= 2-D potential flow), Example 8.3 gravity spreading of a viscous bead (a nonlinear diffusion equation for h) · 8.4 similarity: Stokes' first problem (8.20)–(8.31) (diffusion equation, dimensional analysis, similarity ODE, erf solution, 99 % thickness), the general similarity ansatz (8.32) and Examples 8.4 (Stokes' first problem again), 8.5 (viscous vortex sheet ↔ temporally developing boundary layer, skin friction), 8.6 (decay of a line vortex → Lamb–Oseen), 8.7 (the bead spreads as t^{1/5}) · 8.5 Stokes' second problem (8.33)–(8.38) · 8.6 low-Re flow past a sphere: scalings (8.39)–(8.43), stream-function equation and boundary conditions (8.44)–(8.47), Stokes' solution (8.48)–(8.50), drag (8.51)–(8.52), Millikan's oil drop, the far-field breakdown, Oseen's correction (8.53) · 8.7 final remarks · 38 exercises.
