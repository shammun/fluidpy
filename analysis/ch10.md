## 1. Chapter objectives and outline (our words; what is new vs restated from earlier chapters)

**What the chapter is for (paraphrasing the four objectives):**
1. Show how a computer turns the conservation laws (PDEs) into a finite set of algebraic equations and what can go wrong
   (discretisation, data, boundary and model errors).
2. Build the two classical discretisations from scratch on one model problem — the 1-D convection–diffusion equation — first by
   finite differences (Taylor series → stencils → accuracy, consistency, von Neumann stability, CFL, cell Péclet), then by finite
   elements (weak form → Galerkin → hat functions → mass/stiffness matrices → element assembly).
3. Specify the equations actually used for incompressible viscous flow: the two difficulties (convection-dominated wiggles and the
   incompressibility constraint), and four answers — artificial compressibility, the explicit MacCormack scheme on weakly
   compressible NS, operator splitting (MAC/projection on a staggered grid, Glowinski's Θ-scheme), and mixed finite elements
   (LBB/inf–sup, Taylor–Hood P2–P1, Newton).
4. Demonstrate them on three worked computations — lid-driven cavity (MacCormack vs lattice-Boltzmann data), a square block in a
   channel (drag/lift, shedding, grid convergence), and a cylinder in a channel (mixed FE; Re = 1…100; St) — and end with the
   verification ethic (benchmarks, mesh and time-step refinement, boundary sensitivity).

**Outline with equation ranges:** 10.1 Introduction (error sources, advantages; no equations) · 10.2 Finite differences: model
problem (10.1)–(10.3), Taylor series (10.4)–(10.5), difference formulas (10.6)–(10.8), FTCS (10.9)–(10.11), BTCS (10.12)–(10.13),
convergence (10.14)–(10.15), consistency and truncation error (10.16)–(10.17), von Neumann stability (10.18)–(10.28), upwind and
CFL (10.29)–(10.30), Lax equivalence, cell Péclet (10.31) · 10.3 Finite elements: function spaces (10.32)–(10.33), weak form
(10.34)–(10.38), Galerkin (10.39)–(10.49), matrix form (10.50)–(10.58), hat functions and comparison with FD (10.59)–(10.63),
element view and assembly (10.64)–(10.78) · 10.4 Incompressible viscous flow: NS recap (10.79)–(10.83), convection-dominated
problems (10.84)–(10.94), incompressibility and artificial compressibility (10.95), explicit MacCormack (10.96)–(10.110), MAC
scheme and projection (10.111)–(10.128), Θ-scheme (10.129)–(10.133), mixed FE (10.134)–(10.137) · 10.5 Three examples: cavity
(10.138)–(10.146) + six substeps, Figs. 10.6–10.8; block (10.147)–(10.155), Figs. 10.9–10.14; cylinder by mixed FE
(10.156)–(10.198), Figs. 10.15–10.21 · 10.6 Concluding remarks (verification; other methods) · 6 exercises ((10.199)).

- **SEEN (recap, do not re-derive):** incompressible NS and continuity (10.79)–(10.80) = ch04 (4.39b)/C08; the dimensionless

  Kármán street = ch09 C10; FTCS for pure diffusion and its limit r ≤ ½ = ch01 C12 (P21); Crank–Nicolson/backward Euler =
  ch08 P192–P193; Taylor series = ch01 P26, ch03 P98; Fourier modes = ch05 P142, ch07; iterative Poisson solvers = ch06 C12

  convergence, consistency, stability as three distinct properties; truncation error and the modified equation; von Neumann

- **Climate hooks (for the curator):** the MAC staggering *is* the Arakawa C-grid of ocean and atmosphere models (u, v on faces,
  p/η/h at centres); the CFL condition sets the time step of every explicit weather model (gravity-wave CFL √(gH)Δt/Δx ≤ 1 with
  ch07 speeds); projection = the pressure/Poisson solve of Boussinesq and anelastic models (ch04 C13); numerical diffusion
  0.5 R_cell D is the implicit diffusion of upwind advection schemes in tracer transport; artificial compressibility ≈
  "reduced speed of sound" in some atmospheric models; operator splitting (dynamics/physics) is universal in GCMs.

## 9. Risks, conventions, ambiguities, suspected typos

**Suspected printed slips (each: printed | believed correct | why).**
- **R1 (10.67)–(10.68), element slopes.** Printed: on the element with local nodes x_{A−1}, x_A, dN_A/dx = (2/(x_A − x_{A−1}))dN₁/dξ = −1/(x_A − x_{A−1}) and dN_{A+1}/dx = +1/(x_A − x_{A−1}). The text two lines earlier says N₁ ↔ N_{A−1} and N₂ ↔ N_A on this element, and N_{A+1} is zero there. Correct: dN_{A−1}/dx = −1/hᵉ, dN_A/dx = +1/hᵉ (or relabel the element as [x_A, x_{A+1}] with hᵉ = x_{A+1} − x_A). An index shift; code the consistent version, keep `printed=True`.
- **R2 (p. 435, after (10.73)):** "the nonzero ones require that A = e or e + 1 and B = e or e + 1" contradicts (10.78) (A = e − 1 or e) and Ωᵉ = [x_{A−1}, x_A]. Correct: A, B ∈ {e − 1, e} (node 0 being the Dirichlet node for e = 1, which is why f¹ = −g k¹_{a1}).
- **R3 (after (10.93)):** "a forward-difference scheme is used to discretize the convective term" — T_j − T_{j−1} is a *backward* (upwind for u > 0) difference.
- **R4 (p. 445):** "Therefore p_{0,2} will not appear in equation (10.120)" — (10.120) is the v-predictor; the pressure appears in the Poisson equation (10.124). Correct: "…in equation (10.124)".
- **R5 (cavity Step 5, p. 451):** "2ρⁿ⁺¹ = (ρⁿ + ρ*) − a₁ + [(ρu)*_{i,j} − (ρu)*_{i−1,j}] − …" has a stray "+"; must be −a₁[(ρu)*_{i,j} − (ρu)*_{i−1,j}] (compare (10.106)).
- **R6 (10.172):** the second sum is printed Σ u_{A′}∫N^u_{A′,y}N^p_B; it must be Σ v_{A′} (it comes from ∂v′/∂y in (10.167), and (10.173) pairs it with Bᵀ_{vp}).

- **R8 (p. 458 and the Fig. 10.14 caption):** "the fourth example later in this section" — §10.5 has three examples; the cylinder is the third.
- **R9 (p. 470) Strouhal comparison:** the book's quoted unbounded value at Re = 100 (citing Wen & Lin 2001; value in the private JSON) is about 2 % above the compilations' 0.164–0.165 (Williamson 1996; Kravchenko; Posdziech & Grundmann). **Conventions and symbol collisions (flag in notation.md).**
- **Fourier convention (10.20):** e^{iπkx_i}, so "k" counts half-waves per unit length and θ = kπΔx. Our code uses θ directly and offers `convention="standard"` (e^{ikx}, θ = kΔx).
- **i** is both the grid index and √−1 in (10.20)–(10.24) (roman vs italic in print). Use `1j` and name the index `i`/`j` in code; in text write $\mathrm i$.
- **α, β** have three meanings: FTCS numbers (10.11); Θ-scheme weights (10.129)–(10.133); time-derivative weights (10.163). **θ** = kπΔx (10.26) vs the Θ-scheme fraction. Code names: `alpha_ftcs, beta_ftcs`; `theta_split, alpha_split, beta_split`; `alpha_t, beta_t`.
- **g**: Dirichlet value (10.2), body force per unit mass **g** (10.79), and the y-component g of **g** = (f, g) in (10.119)–(10.120). **D**: diffusivity (10.1), strain-rate tensor D[u] (10.136), block side D and cavity side D (§10.5). **c**: sound speed (10.95), (10.99) vs coefficients c₁–c₅ (10.109) vs FE coefficients c_A (10.45). **M**: mass matrix vs Mach number. **K**: convergence constant (10.15) vs stiffness matrix. **n**: time level, number of elements/nodes, and *frequency* n in S = nd/U (p. 468). **S**: trial space (10.32) vs Strouhal number. **h**: mesh size vs the superscript h. **δ**: layer thickness vs Kronecker δ_AB. **R**: global Péclet (10.87) vs R_cell. **τ**: shedding period. **p′**: Newton correction here (ch04/ch07 used p′ for perturbation pressure).
- **Strouhal**: S = nd/U with the **cyclic** frequency (as ch09); ch04's `strouhal_number(Omega, l, U)` takes Ω — pass 2πn or add a `cyclic=` keyword.
- **Sign of (10.159)/(10.162):** the weak continuity carries a minus sign chosen to make the pressure blocks symmetric (B, Bᵀ); do not "fix" it.
- **Grids:** node-based (§10.2–10.3, cavity/block MacCormack, collocated) vs cell-centred staggered (MAC). Every function says which. The MAC boundary u-faces coincide with the walls; tangential no-slip needs ghost values (a choice the book leaves open — document it).

**Non-dimensionalisations that change.**
§10.2–10.3 dimensional (u [m/s], D [m²/s], L [m]); §10.4 NS dimensionless with Re (10.81); MacCormack (10.103)–(10.109) written
*dimensionally* with μ and c; the cavity algorithm (pp. 451–452) *nondimensional* (lengths D, speed U, time D/U, density ρ₀,
pressure ρ₀U², p = ρ/M²); the block uses the **block side** as length; the FE cylinder the **diameter d** (t̄ = tU/d); force
coefficients normalised by ½ρU²d (per span) and the torque by ½ρU²d². Keep one `Scales` object per example (ch04 pattern).

**Silently switching assumptions / claims stated without derivation.**
- Weakly compressible MacCormack approximates incompressible flow only to O(M²): book Mach numbers (book value, private); ours 0.08 (cavity), 0.06 (block); the Re = 1000 smoke-line case uses a much larger Mach number (Re = 1000
  cylinder — density variations of order M² ≈ 9 % of the dynamic pressure scale; on the edge of "incompressible").
- (10.110) is semi-empirical (not derivable); (10.27) cited to Noye; (10.127)–(10.128) cited to Peyret & Taylor; Θ-scheme order and
  "no loss if u* = u^{n+θ}" cited to Glowinski; LBB stated without the inf–sup inequality; "fully implicit and unconditionally
  stable" for Newton–FE (true for linear model problems, not a theorem for NS).
- "The Galerkin finite-element formulation is equivalent to a finite-difference method" (p. 434) holds for uniform linear elements
  apart from the consistent mass matrix; on nonuniform or higher-order meshes it does not. Our steady-case test shows exact
  equality (the mass matrix drops out when ∂/∂t = 0).
- The MAC scheme is first order in time (splitting error) even though its spatial operators are second order; the Θ-scheme is
  second order only for the special θ.
- Outflow and density boundary conditions of §10.5 are heuristic (the block uses momentum-derived density on the body because it
  "gives better results") — label them as such; the corner treatment (average of two sides) likewise.
- The confined configurations (block and cylinder in channels with sliding walls = a body moving in a channel)
  raise C_D and St above the unbounded values; never compare them numerically with unbounded data.

**Numerical pitfalls to plan for (implementer).**
- `steady_cd_exact` overflows for R ≳ 700 if coded as printed — use the scaled form (ch01/ch07 overflow lesson).
- FTCS demonstrations of instability must cap growth (stop at 1e6 and report) — no NaN-filled plots.
- The pure-Neumann pressure Poisson matrix is singular: pin one value (or add the mean constraint) and check Σ rhs = 0 up to
  round-off; with `splu` factorise once per grid (cache), FFT for periodic tests.
- Runtime: 256² explicit MacCormack to steady state is hours in numpy — the notebook uses 64² (and cached 128²); state the
  resolution beside every comparison with Ghia; never type Ghia numbers into prose (load from `reference/ch10/`).
- Mixed FE unsteady Re = 100 is script-only (tens of minutes) with cached force histories; if the curator wants a live
  shedding demo, the MAC solver with a staircase cylinder is cheaper but its first-order boundary must be stated (ch06 lesson:
  staircase boundaries set the global order).
- Exercise 10.3–10.6 inputs/answers must not appear in public files; the heated-rod series is coded with our own wall
  temperature and scanned against the private JSON.
