Chapter 2 — Cartesian Tensors

Ch. 2 · explainer 1 · §2.2 2.3 2.4 Which rotates — the arrow or the ruler?

Turn the axes under a fixed vector (or a fixed stress state) and watch the direction-cosine matrix C, the new components x' = Cᵀx and the tensor components τ' = CᵀτC move together — one rule applied once per index.

Ch. 2 · explainer 2 · §2.4 2.6 2.11 Cut the point any way you like: what pushes on the cut?

Drag the normal n of a cut through a stressed point: the traction f = n·τ, its normal and shear parts, the Mohr point and the σ_n(φ), τ_s(φ) curves move together; the two shear-free cuts are the eigenvectors, and their normal stresses bound everything.

Ch. 2 · explainer 3 · §2.10, 2.11 Is simple shear a rotation?

A square of fluid carried by u = G·x deforms and spins at once; the same square under S = ½(G + Gᵀ) only stretches, under A = ½(G − Gᵀ) only spins — three panels on one clock, the hidden vector ω of A, and the check that a symmetric tensor cannot see A.

Ch. 2 · explainer 4 · §2.9 2.12 What leaks out of a box?

Move and resize a box over a plane field: the four face fluxes add up to exactly the divergence integrated inside; tile the box and watch the interior faces cancel; shrink it and watch the flux per area settle on the divergence.

Ch. 2 · explainer 5 · §2.9, 2.13 How much does the flow go round a loop?

Drag and resize a loop over a 2-D velocity field: the circulation ∮ u·t ds equals the curl integrated inside (Stokes, Eq. 2.34); a straight shear flow has it, an irrotational vortex has it only when the loop encloses its core; shrink the loop and Γ/A → (∇×u)·n (Eq. 2.35); tile the inside and watch the shared edges cancel.
